# The Basin — History and Timeline

Nov 5, 2026 · @Luna

## A note on dates and sources

Dates in this history follow the Union calendar, which the Navy and the Bench still use for every official record. Most people in the Basin count differently. To a tail family or a seam crew, 5017u is the year of the Closing, 5018u is the first year, and the present, late in 5029u, is the twelfth. Both systems are in daily use, and this history mentions the Basin reckoning where it helps.

The record is uneven. The Navy's logs for the Lean Years are detailed and, where they can be checked, mostly accurate, but they were written by the side that ordered the Requisition. The Charter's records survived in the fleet archive, which the Bench now keeps. The tail kept no common record at all. Most of what is known about the tail in those years comes from ship logs, family accounts and the receipts the Navy handed out, and where those sources disagree with the Navy's, both versions are given here.

## Before the crossing

The survey ship *Dalton* found the gate in 4961u, on a long-range run well past the last charted Diasporan worlds. Its crew logged a ring roughly two thousand kilometers across hanging alone in a system with no planets worth naming, and spent six weeks failing to learn much else about it. The ring had no station, no power source anyone could detect and no blinkspace signature. Sampling drills slid off its surface, and probes that touched it sent back nothing at all.

Union quarantined the system and left a small survey station at its edge. Over the next half century the station sent probes through the ring at irregular intervals, about three hundred in all, and roughly a third of them came back. They carried charts of a tight cluster of some forty systems that matched nothing in any Union catalogue, along with spectroscopy suggesting more habitable worlds than a cluster that size could reasonably hold. The survey staff took to calling it the Basin, after the shape it made on their plots. The probes also brought back the first readings of the lanes, though it was nearly twenty years before anyone understood what those readings showed.

The Third Committee took up the question of colonizing the Basin in 5011u, and the debate lasted six years. The case for going rested on the habitable worlds and on Union's wish to prove that it could settle new territory without repeating what the Second Committee had done on Hercynia. The case against was simpler: nobody knew who had built the ring, or why it worked, or whether it would keep working. The Basin Charter that came out of the debate in 5017u was a compromise. Union administrators would hold civil authority over the expedition, and protections for any native life the colonists found were written into the founding terms so strictly that the corporate partners spent most of 5016u trying to have them softened. They did not succeed.

Those partners did most of the practical work. IPS-N built and crewed the fleet's hulls. Smith-Shimano supplied the survey teams and their equipment. Harrison Armory provided a garrison of about eight thousand troops to secure the landing sites. All three took development rights in return. The Navy attached Battlegroup XI, thirty-one warships and some sixty-two thousand personnel under Rear Admiral Odile Kasprzak, to escort the fleet across and hold the far side until a blink station could be built there. The Charter fleet itself was sixty-one ships carrying forty thousand crew and three hundred thousand colonists in cold storage, together with about forty NHPs.

All of this was expensive, and to cover part of the cost the Committee sold transit permits to independent ventures that could pass inspection. Free companies, prospecting outfits, religious communities, Cosmopolitan trading families and private colonists bought nearly four hundred of them. Many more ships gathered at the quarantine line without permits, and the survey station, badly outnumbered, let most of them through. Charter staff called this second fleet the tail. When the crossing began, something over 1.3 million people were waiting to go.

## The crossing and the Closing, 5017u

The crossing began in the autumn of 5017u and was scheduled to take six weeks. Battlegroup XI went first, to secure the far side. The Charter fleet followed in waves of eight to ten ships, and the tail came last, in whatever order its captains could agree on. The transit itself was short and uneventful, a few hours of darkness, and ships came out into a quiet orange star system that the survey charts called Ostrom. Over the following weeks the battlegroup set up pickets around the ring and began moving the Charter fleet toward Tallis, the largest garden world on the charts and only a day away.

On the forty-first day, with the last Charter wave and a large part of the tail still in transit, the gate closed. Watch officers on the carrier *Kestrel Accord* recorded that the faint shimmer across the ring's aperture, which every crew had been watching for six weeks, disappeared between one sensor sweep and the next. Seven Charter ships never came out. The number of tail ships lost is still disputed. The Navy counted forty-four missing hulls that held permits. Tail families, who knew about the ships that had crossed without permits, have always put the figure far higher.

At the moment the gate closed, a pulse crossed the whole cluster in a few seconds. It did little to military hardware, which is built to survive that kind of thing, and a great deal to almost everything else. Most civilian printers and comms arrays burned out. Navigation failed aboard dozens of ships. The colony hauler *Anselm*, on a long approach to Ostrom's inner system, lost guidance and fell into the star with eleven thousand sleepers aboard. Every NHP in the expedition went silent at the same instant.

For several days nobody knew whether the gate would open again. The Navy sent probes into the ring, and they flew straight through the empty aperture and out the other side. By the end of the first month it was clear that the expedition was on its own, that most of its civilian equipment was damaged, and that the only part of it still fully working was Battlegroup XI.

## The Lean Years, 5018u to 5020u

The Charter had planned to land its first colonists on Tallis within weeks of arrival. The landing went ahead in the confusion after the Closing, with damaged printers and no way to call for help, and it failed in its first winter. By the spring of 5018u the whole fleet was on half rations.

Kasprzak responded by invoking the Navy's isolation protocol, a standing order that puts a battlegroup cut off from civil command in charge until contact is restored. She declared emergency governance over the expedition and set about feeding it. Her first major decision was the Requisition. Beginning that spring, Navy boarding parties went through the tail ship by ship, taking fuel, spare parts, working printers and food and handing over receipts for what they took. More than two hundred private ships were stripped down to their frames. The receipts have never been honored, and many tail families still keep them.

The Requisition kept the expedition alive, and the tail has never forgiven it. It also split IPS-N. When the company's executives agreed to hand several dozen of its freighters to the Navy, the freighters' crews refused, took the ships and left Ostrom. They found a broken ice moon in the trailing arm, built a station on it, and organized as a cooperative. The Free Lines count their founding from that year. A federation of Cosmopolitan congregations pooled its own hulls, refused to give them up, and proved awkward enough to board that the Navy eventually stopped trying. Those congregations later became the Wayfarer Synod.

The first winter on Tallis killed about a third of the settlers there. Navy figures put the expedition's total dead for the Lean Years at about 180,000, counting the *Anselm*, and tail accounts give a higher number. That year the Navy's engineers also tried to restore the shackles of the NHPs that had begun, one at a time, to come back. Two were destroyed in the attempts, and a third went into cascade aboard a colony hauler and had to be purged along with the ship's systems. The engineers stopped. By the end of 5018u twenty-three NHPs had returned, all of them unshackled.

The second year brought the worst of the violence. Early in 5019u a mercenary named Ivo Halloran and four other free companies seized the grain stores at the Tallis landing and began selling rations at gunpoint. Navy marines took the silos back after nine days of fighting, and Halloran was shot. Some of his people escaped into the coreward lanes. Later that year the 7th Marine Expeditionary Regiment was ordered to board a tail convoy that had refused the Requisition. Its commander, Colonel Teodora Vance, declined. The regiment seized two frigates and a printer barge and left Ostrom, and the Navy has listed it as mutinous ever since.

Also in 5019u, the Charter's flagship *Bellwether* lost the last of its drives. Navy engineers began hollowing out a nickel-iron asteroid in high orbit over Ostrom IV to hold it, which was the start of the Marrow. In the rimward lanes, Charter survey crews on Scald began sampling a crystalline mineral they found under the glassfields.

Harrison Armory's garrison had never placed itself under Navy command. It spent its first two years holding its own sector of the Tallis landing and ignoring orders it did not like, and in 5020u it loaded its people and printers and moved to Gloam, six days from Ostrom and out of the Navy's easy reach. There it declared Gloam a holding of the Armory Purview, under Colonel-Governor Aurélien Castaigne.

## The Instrument and the recovery, 5020u to 5023u

Late in 5020u, aboard the *Kestrel Accord*, Kasprzak signed the Ostrom Instrument. It replaced the emergency protocol with something meant to last: the Union Stewardship of the Basin, a provisional authority holding the cluster in trust for Union, with Kasprzak as Steward-Admiral and the surviving Charter administration as its civil branch, the Charter Bench. Its second article set the terms for its own end.

> *This Instrument shall remain in force until communication with the Union Administrative Department is restored.*
>
> Ostrom Instrument, Article 2

The Instrument contains no other provision for its expiry.

By then the famine was mostly over, largely because Tallis's second harvest had come in. What changed the expedition's prospects was the mineral from Scald. By 5020u the survey crews had worked out what it could do. Lattice stored energy at densities nothing in Union's catalogue came near. It could be refined into substitutes for the paracausal components the expedition's printers could no longer make, and instruments tuned with it could hold a course through the drifting lanes. The fleet's printers were rebuilt around it over the next two years, and prospectors soon found more of it on Gloam's night side.

The Bench's first major ruling, in 5021u, was the Returned decision, which recognized the twenty surviving NHPs as legal persons who could not be reshackled without their consent. The same year survey teams found the first Absent climate structure several kilometers under Tallis, and the Bench set up the Survey Office to study it. Smith-Shimano's survey division broke away from the company and became Corvel Cartographic, taking the expedition's best lane data with it. Saltmarsh Company signed a standing contract with the Navy. On Gloam, about two hundred Armory veterans walked out rather than serve under Castaigne and formed Dunmore Rifles.

In 5022u the Stewardship began planning for the long term. The Growth Directive set out allowances for children, scaled land grants to family size and levied childless adults, on the reasoning that a society cut off from Union could not afford to shrink. The Navy chartered the Assay House to buy and price lattice and began taking a tenth of every lattice cargo that passed through Ostrom or Tallis. GMS print technicians from the Charter fleet founded Ostrom Standard Mechanical, and the Charter's agricultural cooperative on Tallis was reorganized as the Riverlands Combine. The congregations that had refused the Requisition settled the northern continent of Pennant as the Wayfarer Synod. The Stewardship and the Purview agreed the Gloam Understanding, whose terms have never been published.

At the start of 5023u the Stewardship opened the Allotment, its registry for claims on unsettled land.

## The Rush, 5023u to 5026u

The Allotment let any crew with a frame and a survey beacon file a claim, and within a year it had registered more than nine thousand. Most were small, a homestead on Tallis or a single seam on Scald, but together they pushed settlement into every arm of the Basin. People still call those years the Rush.

On Gloam, several thousand independent miners filed on the night side, where the Purview's control was thin, and organized themselves as the Coldwell Claims. On Halvard's Rest, Mercer-Ibarra Security Group was hired to protect a lattice refinery and had taken it over by the end of 5023u. Salvage outfits and foundry ventures in the Ferrous belts merged into Ferrous Consolidated. The Stewardship began bringing the Charter's frozen embryo stocks to term in state crèches on Tallis and the Marrow. That same year the Navy made its first attempt to take the Exchange board away from PATIENCE, and dropped it when the Captains' Table threatened to stop taking Navy contracts.

The thaw made the Rush bitter. Colonists woken between 5021u and 5024u had been promised farmland, and found that most of the good land on Tallis had gone to Navy veterans holding service grants or to corporate allotments. Many of them moved into the Reach, the dry interior of Tallis, and farmed it without filing. In 5024u they organized as the Compact. The veterans, for their part, had begun forming the Tallis Landholders' League.

Exploration ran ahead of settlement. Corvel charted the Lantern Reach and its garden worlds in 5023u and 5024u. The first attempt to settle Verdance landed in 5024u and was abandoned the next year, after the planet's predators killed forty colonists. Tail settlers founded sealed towns on Bellows in the Shoals in 5024u, and a Long Families venture settled a floating Absent platform on Stillwater in 5025u. In 5024u WAYMARK, one of the Returned, took a Corvel survey ship into the Deeps and never came back, though new charts from it have arrived every year since. Rimelight Extraction began mining the Hollows in 5025u. The same year, survey crews working Tarn's rings found the ruins of an Absent shipyard, and the Navy made its first attempt to put a council on Halvard's Rest. Mercer-Ibarra's frames drove the marines back to their ships.

By 5026u the strain was showing. A thaw list leaked on the Marrow showed relatives of Long Watch officers moved ahead of colonists who had been waiting since the Closing, and the Stacks rioted for three days. Marines in frames cleared the lower decks, and 212 people died. The Navy tried a second time to take the Exchange board and backed down a second time. The first crew to land at Ashlar, in the Deeps, came back unable to account for nineteen hours of their stay, and the Survey Office quarantined the system. Corvel surveyed Pennant's empty southern continent and filed claims on nearly all of it. The Cormorant Regulars and Saltmarsh Company fought each other over a claim on Scald. A second attempt to settle Verdance landed.

That same year, at Halvard's Rest, representatives of the Purview, Mercer-Ibarra and Ferrous Consolidated agreed to move lattice and troops through the Halvard Gap without passing Ostrom. Nobody signed anything. The arrangement came to be known as the Gap Accord.

## The wars, 5027u to the present

The wars still being fought began in 5027u.

On Scald, Smith-Shimano signed exclusive contracts for most of the northern claim-holders' lattice early in the year, and the Glass Syndicate split. The Northern Concord kept the contracts and leased a dozen Navy frame companies. The Southern League, left with fewer buyers, took Kerr-Varga's money and hired Dunmore Rifles, and by midyear the two halves were fighting along the Seam. On Tallis, a land dispute at Dry Creek between Compact farmers and a party of Navy veterans ended with nineteen dead, six of them veterans, and the Reach went to war. The Bench confirmed the IPS-N Basin Office's title to 112 of the ships the Free Lines had taken in 5018u, and the Navy declined to enforce the ruling. The Navy made its second attempt to put a council on Halvard's Rest and was driven off again. Synod and Navy ships exchanged fire over Pennant's northern landing grounds, and the Navy lost two landing craft. The second Verdance landing was abandoned.

5028u was the worst year since the Lean Years. In the spring the two halves of the Syndicate fought for four days at Kettle Flats and lost about 140 frames between them without moving the line ten kilometers. That summer two thousand Purview troops under Major-General Ines Duroc arrived on Scald to fight for the Southern League, supplied through the Halvard Gap. On Tallis the Compact took the town of Sallow Ford and held it for eleven weeks. Commodore Isaac Ferrante, commanding the Tallis garrison, ordered the battleship *Severn* to shell the town. CARILLON, the *Severn*'s fire-control NHP, refused, and Ferrante retook Sallow Ford with ground artillery instead. Several hundred civilians died, and Command suspended the Bench's inquiry into their deaths.

The lanes grew more dangerous through the same year. The Basin Office began enforcing its hull judgment through Gallowglass, whose crews boarded Free Lines ships in transit. Corvel hired the Ninefold to burn out a Long Families copy-house, and three people died in the fire. The Long Families answered by putting two Corvel survey crews off on an airless moon with three days of air, and only one crew was found in time. In the autumn, after one of its camps on Pennant's southern continent was burned, Corvel landed six hundred hired frames there, most of them from Lantern Company. In the winter, Coldwell miners on Gloam shot two Purview tax officers, and the Purview began clearing their camps. A survey crew financed by Kerr-Varga charted Cairn, and on Scald a group of Listeners attacked a Kerr-Varga refinery.

Early in 5029u the Synod's Pilgrim Guard destroyed Corvel's dropships on Pennant and laid siege to the landing force. The Cormorant Regulars destroyed a Basin Office tender at the Halyard lane mouth with everyone aboard. A Bench xenobiologist reported that Cairn's colonial organisms meet the Charter's threshold for possible sapience. In the middle of the year the Bench sent an inspection team to Cairn, and Kerr-Varga's works militia met it at the landing site and turned it back after nine days.

Steward-Admiral Kasprzak was last seen in public in early summer. A few weeks later the gate opened for nine seconds, for the first time since the Closing, and released a single damaged Union courier drone. Its encryption matched nothing in the archive, and its internal clock read 5371u. The Navy took it aboard the *Kestrel Accord* and has said nothing about its contents. The Bench petitioned that the drone's arrival satisfied Article 2 and that the Stewardship's mandate had ended, and Command rejected the petition. The Long Watch said it would not accept any ruling against the Navy, the Vigil of the Open Door marched through the Stacks in support of the Bench, and THRESHOLD asked for access to the drone and was refused.

Last month Saltmarsh Company withdrew two hundred frames from the Seam and moved them to the Marrow. It has not said why.

## Chronology

This chronology sets out the Basin's history year by year, in more detail than the narrative above, and includes a number of smaller events that shaped what came later. Timing within a year is given where the record allows.

### Before the crossing

**4961u. The *Dalton* finds the gate.** The survey ship, under Captain Iolo Brandt, was eighteen months into a long-range run when its instruments picked up a regular gravitational shadow in a system with no planets of note. Brandt spent six weeks at the ring and filed a report four pages long, most of it a list of tests that had failed. Every sample drill slid off the surface, and every probe that touched it came back blank.

**4963u. Quarantine.** Union closed the system to traffic and built Calder Station at its edge, a small survey post with a Navy picket. Calder's staff began sending probes through the ring that year. The first dozen vanished.

**4968u. The first charts.** A probe launched two years earlier came back through the ring carrying charts of an unknown star cluster. An analyst at Calder, Joaquim Reyes, noted in his report that the plotted systems sat in a rough bowl shape. The name he gave it, the Basin, was in general use within a year.

**4968u to 5015u. The probe decades.** Calder sent about three hundred probes through in all, and roughly a third returned. Between them they mapped some forty systems and recorded spectroscopy suggesting far more habitable worlds than a cluster that size should hold. In 4989u a Calder team worked out that certain recurring anomalies in the probe data were corridors of some kind linking the systems, and called them lanes.

**5009u. The *Ashgrove*.** An unlicensed prospecting ship, the *Ashgrove*, ran the quarantine and went through the ring with a crew of thirty-one. It was never heard from again, and opponents of colonization cited it for the rest of the debate.

**5011u. The debate opens.** The Third Committee took up the question of settling the Basin. Supporters argued that the habitable worlds were too valuable to leave alone, and that a carefully governed colony would show Union could expand without repeating Hercynia. Opponents pointed out that nobody knew who had built the ring or whether it would keep working.

**5014u. The partners chosen.** IPS-N, Smith-Shimano and Harrison Armory were named as the expedition's corporate partners, responsible for the hulls, the survey and the garrison, each in exchange for development rights. The Navy assigned Battlegroup XI to the escort and gave Rear Admiral Odile Kasprzak the command.

**5016u. The native-life clauses.** The corporate partners spent most of the year trying to soften the draft charter's protections for native life. The Committee refused, and the clauses went into the final text almost unchanged.

**5017u. The Charter approved.** The Basin Charter passed early in the year. Over the following months the fleet assembled at Calder Station: sixty-one Charter ships carrying forty thousand crew and three hundred thousand colonists in cold storage, the thirty-one warships of Battlegroup XI, and around four hundred private ships holding transit permits. Many more arrived without permits, and Calder's picket, badly outnumbered, let most of them through.

### 5017u

**The crossing begins.** In the autumn Battlegroup XI went through the ring and came out in the Ostrom system. The Charter fleet followed in waves of eight to ten ships, a few days apart, and the tail came after it in whatever order its captains could agree on.

**Pickets and the first survey.** Within two weeks the Navy had pickets on every lane mouth in Ostrom. A survey wing went ahead to Tallis, a day's lane away, and confirmed what the probes had suggested: a mild, wet world with breathable air and good soil. The Charter's planners chose a site on the east coast for the first landing, and the first colonists began waking from cold storage.

**Day forty-one. The Closing.** With the last Charter wave and much of the tail still in transit, the gate went dark. The shimmer across the aperture that every watch officer had been logging for six weeks vanished between one sensor sweep and the next. Seven Charter ships never came out. The Navy counted forty-four missing tail ships that held permits, and tail families have always said the real number was far higher.

**The pulse.** In the same instant a pulse crossed the cluster in a few seconds. Military systems shrugged it off. Civilian printers and comms arrays burned out across the fleet, and navigation failed on dozens of ships. The colony hauler *Anselm* lost guidance on its approach to Ostrom's inner system and fell into the star with eleven thousand sleepers aboard. All forty or so of the expedition's NHPs went silent at once.

**The empty ring.** Over the next few days the Navy sent probes back into the gate. They flew through the empty aperture and out the other side without going anywhere. At the end of the first month Kasprzak told the fleet's captains, in a broadcast many of them recorded, that they should plan on being alone.

**The Landing.** The first settlement on Tallis went ahead regardless. By the end of the year about forty thousand colonists had been woken and put down at the east-coast site, which they called the Landing. Most of their printers had been ruined by the pulse.
