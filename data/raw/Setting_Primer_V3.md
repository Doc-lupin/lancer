# The Basin — Setting Primer V3

Nov 4, 2026 · @Luna

## The short version

In 5017u about 1.3 million people went through an alien gate on the far edge of Union space, aboard a Union colonial fleet and the hundreds of private ships that followed it. Forty-one days into the crossing the gate shut behind them, and it stayed shut for twelve years. Four months ago it opened for nine seconds and released a single damaged Union courier drone whose clock reads 5371u. Nobody on this side yet knows what that means.

The Basin is what lies beyond the gate: a dense cluster of more than a hundred stars, joined by corridors called lanes that a vanished people built and nobody understands. The colonists call the builders the Absent. Their machinery keeps a handful of garden worlds green, and their ruins are scattered across the cluster. The mineral that seems to have powered it all, lattice, now runs nearly everything people have built here, and it is found mostly in places nobody would choose to live.

The expedition has become a frontier civilization of about 1.2 million people awake and 140,000 still asleep, growing quickly and fighting constantly. The Union Navy battlegroup that escorted the fleet now governs as the Stewardship from Ostrom and Tallis, the crossroads where the Basin's four arms meet. Around it are an Armory holding, a theocracy, a lattice syndicate at war with itself, company towns, shipping cooperatives, settler insurgents, mercenaries, pirates and twenty unshackled NHPs. There is work for anyone who can pilot a frame.

This primer gives the broad picture. The [official map](https://claude.ai/artifact/9DqroZ6uXdHPxxTesEvHZs), the [geography](https://claude.ai/artifact/3xU7uFycuu7xjzi6VDGvFV) and the [factions](https://claude.ai/artifact/Q4z9kZtmMiVQv7FZxDYk9T) go into detail.

## How it happened

### The gate and the Charter

The survey ship *Dalton* found the gate in 4961u, far past the last charted Diasporan worlds, in a system with nothing else in it worth naming. It is a ring about two thousand kilometers across, with no station, no power source anyone has found and no blinkspace signature. Union quarantined the system and spent half a century sending probes through. About a third came back, carrying charts of a tight cluster of some forty systems that appeared in no Union catalogue and held more habitable worlds than a cluster that size had any right to. The survey staff called it the Basin.

The Third Committee approved the Basin Charter in 5017u. Union administrators held civil authority, and protections for native life were written into the founding terms with Hercynia in mind. IPS-N supplied the hulls, Smith-Shimano the survey and Harrison Armory a security garrison, each in return for development rights, and the Navy attached Battlegroup XI to escort the fleet and hold the far side until a blink station could be built. The Charter fleet was sixty-one ships carrying forty thousand crew and three hundred thousand colonists in cold storage. To help pay for it, the Committee sold transit permits, and nearly four hundred private ships followed the fleet through, along with others that never bothered with permits. Charter staff called them the tail.

### The Closing

The battlegroup crossed first, then the Charter fleet, then the tail. On the forty-first day the gate went dark with seven Charter ships and an unknown number of tail hulls still inside it, and none of them has been seen since. A pulse swept the cluster at the same moment. It burned out most civilian printers and comms arrays, sent the colony hauler *Anselm* into Ostrom's star with eleven thousand sleepers aboard, and silenced every NHP in the expedition. Navy hulls are built to take that kind of damage and came through almost untouched, and for most of the following year the battlegroup was the only part of the expedition that still worked.

### The Lean Years

The first landing on Tallis failed in its first winter. By spring the fleet was on half rations, and Rear Admiral Odile Kasprzak invoked the Navy's isolation protocol and took charge. Her first major act was the Requisition. Navy boarding parties stripped the tail of fuel, parts, printers and food, paying in receipts that have never been honored, and more than two hundred private ships were left as empty hulls. When IPS-N's executives agreed to hand several dozen company freighters to the Navy as well, the crews took the ships and left. They became the Free Lines.

The next three years are remembered as the Lean Years, and a third of the Tallis settlers died in the first of them. In 5019u a mercenary named Ivo Halloran seized the grain stores at the Tallis landing; Navy marines retook them nine days later and Halloran was shot. That same year the 7th Marine Expeditionary Regiment refused orders to board a tail convoy, left Ostrom with two frigates, and has lived outside the law ever since. In 5020u Harrison Armory's garrison, which had never accepted Navy command, moved to Gloam and declared it an Armory holding.

### The Instrument and the Rush

Late in 5020u, aboard the carrier *Kestrel Accord*, Kasprzak signed the Ostrom Instrument. It made the expedition the Union Stewardship of the Basin, a provisional authority holding the cluster in trust for Union, with the surviving Charter administration as its civil branch.

> *This Instrument shall remain in force until communication with the Union Administrative Department is restored.*
>
> Ostrom Instrument, Article 2

The famine ended. The Marrow was finished around the old Charter flagship, Absent machinery was found under Tallis, and the Navy chartered the Assay House to buy and price lattice. In 5023u the Stewardship opened the Allotment, its registry for claims on unsettled land, and the flood of claim-filers that followed is still called the Rush. In the years since, the expedition's corporate partners have broken apart and been joined by new companies, mercenaries have become governments, settlers have pushed into every arm, and the Basin has fought a string of corporate and colonial wars that shows no sign of ending.

## The shape of the Basin

The Basin is about fifty light-years across, a rough sphere of more than a hundred stars. Spacers divide it into regions: groups of stars, anywhere from two to a dozen, linked by local lanes a few hours long. Between regions run the primary lanes, which take anywhere from a day to nearly three weeks. It is the lanes that give the Basin its shape, and charted, that shape is four arms of very different size meeting at a crossroads.

Ostrom and Tallis sit at the crossroads, a day apart. Ostrom holds the gate and the Marrow, and its lanes run spinward to Gloam and rimward to Scald. Tallis, the largest garden world in the Basin, opens coreward toward Pennant and trailing toward Halyard. Almost any journey from one arm to another passes through one or both of them.

The **coreward arm** is the greenest. It holds the forested garden world of Pennant, the failing garden world of Orison and the ocean world of Wending, with the garden worlds of the Lantern Reach, the dead stars of the Pale and the Absent clockwork of the Orrery beyond them. The **trailing arm** is a tight commercial knot of three regions only a day or two apart, the ice fields of Halyard, the brown dwarf Lumen and the free port of Sable, opening onto the Furnace nebula and the starless worlds of the Hollows. The **spinward arm** is long and thin: Gloam, tidally locked and six days from Ostrom, then Halvard and its gas giant, then the tangled lanes of the Shoals. The **rimward arm** is industrial and rough. Scald's glassfields hold the richest easily reached lattice in the Basin, with the foundries of Ferrous and the ringed world of Tarn on either side, and lonely Cairn on the road out to the Deeps, the Boneyard and the Mire.

A few routes link neighboring arms without passing the crossroads, and each is held by someone the Stewardship would rather not depend on. The Orison tide joins the coreward and spinward arms, the Wending passage the coreward and trailing arms, and the Sable Run and the one-way Hollows loop the trailing and rimward arms. The Halvard Gap links spinward to rimward. Opposite arms have no bypass at all.

Not all lanes behave alike. Stable lanes hold their course year to year. Drifting lanes wander over weeks or months and need lattice-tuned navigation and a recent chart. Tidal lanes open for about two weeks in every seventy-three days, in step with the pulse of the Lantern Reach's stars. One-way lanes take ships in one direction only. Silent lanes, like the three Silent Mouths in Ostrom, are charted mouths that nothing has ever come back through.

## The Absent and lattice

Nobody has found a body, a grave or anything recognizable as writing left by the Absent. What they left is machinery, and some of it is still running. Every primary lane is theirs, and so is the gate. So is the machinery buried kilometers beneath Tallis, Pennant and the garden worlds of the Lantern Reach that regulates their air and climate. Elsewhere they left stranger things: the empty stone towers of Ashlar in the Deeps, nine worlds circling a single structure in the Orrery, the ruined shipyard in Tarn's rings, the moon-sized wrecks of the Boneyard and the heat engines that keep the rogue worlds of the Hollows warm. Below about four hundred meters on Scald and Gloam, mining crews have found machines that are still awake and attack anyone who digs too close. The Navy calls them custodians.

Lattice is the mineral that makes the Basin run. It stores energy at densities nothing in Union's catalogue approaches, it stands in for the paracausal components the expedition can no longer make, and lattice-tuned instruments are the only safe way through a drifting lane. Almost every frame, ship and printer in the Basin depends on it. Large quantities brought close to the gate also produce measurable changes on its surface, and every serious attempt to reopen the gate has started there.

Lattice is found almost everywhere people would rather not live. The garden worlds have little or none. The rich deposits are on Gloam's frozen night side, under Scald's glassfields, in the Shoals, the Furnace and the Mire, and above all in the Hollows. The most widely held theory is that the Absent's climate engines draw lattice out of the worlds they regulate, which would explain why small seams have started appearing on Orison as its machinery fails. Ostrom has small deposits of its own. The Stewardship's real wealth in lattice comes from the crossroads, where it takes a tenth of every cargo that crosses.

## The Marrow

Every arm of the Basin leads back to the crossroads, and most traffic at the crossroads ends up at the Marrow. It is the Basin's capital, its main port and its largest market.

It began in 5019u as somewhere to put the *Bellwether*, the Charter's flagship, after its drives failed. Navy engineers hollowed out a nickel-iron asteroid in high orbit over Ostrom IV, towed the ship inside and built around it. A dozen hulls stripped in the Requisition were welded on as extra decks, a docking ring went up big enough to take the *Kestrel Accord*, and housing kept being added wherever there was room and air. About two hundred thousand people live aboard now.

Ships dock at the Hangar Ring, which Stewardship marines run. Every hull is swept there for undeclared lattice and unregistered NHPs, and every cargo pays its levy. Inside the ring is the Exchange, four kilometers of showrooms, brokers, bars and assay offices, with the Assay House at one end posting the lattice price the whole Basin quotes. The Exchange board, run by the NHP PATIENCE, carries most of the hired work in the Basin. The Stacks, where most of the station's people live, fill the asteroid's outer layers and are badly mapped even by station management. Command runs along the spine and holds the Stewardship's offices, the Bench's courts and the Allotment registry.

The *Bellwether* sits at the center, and its holds, now called the Cold Hold, still keep 140,000 colonists asleep. On the outer hull, facing the gate, the Vigil of the Open Door keeps its watch.

## The powers

Nobody rules the whole Basin, and nobody has a monopoly on force. The [factions](https://claude.ai/artifact/Q4z9kZtmMiVQv7FZxDYk9T) document covers more than forty powers in detail. This is the outline.

### The Stewardship

The Stewardship is the strongest power by a wide margin. It holds Ostrom and Tallis outright and fields the only capital ships in the Basin, the carrier *Kestrel Accord* and the battleships *Severn* and *Coriolis*, along with twenty-four other warships and some 120,000 people under arms. Its pickets at Cinder and Brace tax nearly everything that moves between the arms. A tenth of every lattice cargo that crosses goes into its reserve, and the Assay House it chartered sets the Basin's lattice price, so the Navy has never been short of it. Its reach thins a few days past its pickets, though, and away from the crossroads it works through contractors such as Saltmarsh Company, through treaties, and through other powers' need for its markets.

Steward-Admiral Odile Kasprzak has led it since the Closing. Her officers are split between the Long Watch, who want the Stewardship made permanent, and the Restorationists, who want civil government back once the Basin is stable. Its civil branch, the Charter Bench, runs the courts, the Allotment and the archive of manufacturer licenses from which every legal frame in the Basin is printed.

### The three alignments

Most other powers have settled into one of three loose alignments, defined by which routes and markets they depend on.

**The crossroads** is the Stewardship and the powers that live off its markets: the Assay House, the IPS-N Basin Office and its Ostrom shipyards, Ostrom Standard Mechanical, the Riverlands Combine that feeds the inner Basin from Tallis, and the Tallis Landholders' League of Navy veterans. The Northern Concord, the northern half of Scald's Glass Syndicate, sells to them under long contracts.

**The Gap Accord** is the crossroads' main rival, an informal partnership of the powers that move lattice and troops through the Halvard Gap without passing Ostrom. At its center is the Gloam Purview, the Harrison Armory holding on Gloam, with twenty thousand troops, a squadron of eight warships and rich lattice under its night side. Mercer-Ibarra, the mercenary company that governs Halvard's Rest, holds the Gap itself. Ferrous Consolidated, whose foundries make most of the Basin's printer feed, sits at its far end, and Kerr-Varga Lattice Works and the Southern League on Scald round it out.

**The trailing traders** are the Free Lines, the shipping cooperative that carries most of the Basin's freight from Halyard Station, the Cosmopolitan Long Families and their armed convoy, and the free port of Sable. They have the most ships and the least land. Smith-Shimano, at Lumen, builds the Basin's best frames and sells to every side without belonging to any.

### Outside the alignments

The Wayfarer Synod, a theocracy of seventy thousand on Pennant, holds the only lane into the Lantern Reach and has turned Navy patrols away twice. The Orison Assembly is trying to evacuate a dying world. Corvel Cartographic sells the charts everyone needs and is trusted by nobody. The Compact, a settler insurgency in the interior of Tallis, fights the crossroads from inside it, and the Coldwell Claims fight the Purview on Gloam's night side. About two hundred free companies hire out to all of them.

The Basin's outlaws include the Seventh, a mutinous marine regiment hiding in the Shoals; the Unlit, raiders who run dark along the rimward frontier; the Tallymen, who sell protection on the Sable Run; the Ringwreckers in Tarn's rings; and Halloran's Heirs, who prey on shipping in the coreward arm.

## The Returned

About forty NHPs crossed the gate. The Closing silenced all of them, and the twenty-three that came back did so without their shackles. Twenty survive. The Bench's Returned decision of 5021u made them legal persons who may not be reshackled without their consent. Any NHP made in the Basin since then must be shackled and registered with the Navy, and running one unregistered is a capital crime. Union doctrine holds that an unshackled NHP will eventually cascade. None of the Returned has shown the recognized signs in twelve years, and most of them spend a great deal of time near lattice.

They do not act together, but several are powers in their own right. PATIENCE runs the Exchange board on the Marrow. LONGSHORE plans the Free Lines' convoys from a seat on their council. CARILLON directs the guns of the battleship *Severn*, and last year it refused to fire on Sallow Ford. THRESHOLD has spent a decade studying the gate from a platform near the ring. SEXTANT advises the Synod, ORDINAL serves with the Seventh, and ARBITRAGE keeps the Glass Syndicate's registry while both halves of the Syndicate try to steal it. WAYMARK is somewhere in the Deeps, and five more have not reported their whereabouts in years.

## Living in the Basin

### Work

Most people in the Basin farm, mine, build, haul or fight, and plenty do several of those in a single year. The economy runs on contracts, posted on the Exchange board and on dozens of smaller boards across the arms. Exploration is a trade like any other. A survey contract pays a crew to chart a lane and catalogue whatever lies at the far end of it. A settlement contract pays them to deliver colonists, prefab and a printer to a new site and hold it through the first year, which usually means fighting someone for it. Claims are filed with the Allotment and last as long as their holders can defend them. The seam crews who mine lattice on Scald, Gloam and the Shoals earn the best pay in the Basin and die at the highest rate.

### Prints

Almost everything that matters is printed: frames, weapons, tools, ship parts, housing. The fleet's printers were rebuilt after the Closing with lattice in place of the paracausal components nobody can make anymore, and what a printer can produce depends on the licenses its owner holds. The master copies sit in the Bench's archive. Copies are sold, leased, inherited, stolen and pirated, and the Navy's Registry of Prints certifies new designs for sale. Unsigned prints, code of uncertain origin that runs on almost any printer, circulate everywhere, mostly by way of the Long Families and Sable. A pilot's license holdings are often worth more than the frame they happen to be flying.

### Peoples

People are still sorted, roughly, by how they came through the gate. Charter folk brought the Third Committee's Union with them and staff most of the Bench and the Navy's older officer corps. The tail, about nine hundred thousand strong at the crossing, run most of the independent trade and have not forgotten the Requisition. The Thawed, woken from cold storage into a Basin at war, make up much of the Stacks and the Compact. A fifth of the population was born in the Basin, none of them older than twelve, and to them Union is a story their parents tell.

### Growth and the thaw

For all the fighting, the population is rising fast, from about 870,000 awake at the end of the Lean Years to nearly 1.2 million now. Under the Stewardship's Growth Directive of 5022u, households receive allowances for every child, service land grants grow with family size, childless adults over thirty-five pay a levy, and the Charter's frozen embryo stocks are being brought to term in state crèches. The Purview and the Synod run their own versions.

About 140,000 colonists are still asleep in the Cold Hold, and the Navy decides who wakes. Relatives of serving personnel go first, then people with skills the Stewardship needs, then everyone else in boarding order, at a rate of about four thousand a year. At that pace the last of them will wake around 5064u. A leaked thaw list set off three days of riots in the Stacks in 5026u, and marines in frames killed 212 people clearing the lower decks.

### Faiths

The Wayfarer Synod is the largest church in the Basin and governs Pennant. The Vigil of the Open Door, a Union civic faith that keeps watch on the gate, has tripled in size since the drone came through. The Gardeners believe the Absent's machinery was left in trust and that lattice mining is starving it, and a militant few have begun attacking lattice shipments. The Listeners, communities of miners who believe the Absent speak through the lattice, have sabotaged deep extraction sites on Scald.

## The wars

There is fighting somewhere in the Basin every day. Most of it is small, a claim jumped or a convoy hit. These are the larger conflicts.

**The Glassfields War.** Since 5027u the two halves of Scald's Glass Syndicate have fought along the Seam, a six-hundred-kilometer front across open glass. The Northern Concord has Smith-Shimano's contracts, a dozen leased Navy frame companies and Saltmarsh. The Southern League has Kerr-Varga's money, Dunmore Rifles and two thousand Purview troops supplied through the Halvard Gap. Thirty or forty free companies are under contract on Scald at any one time. The fighting at Kettle Flats in 5028u cost about 140 frames in four days and moved the line less than ten kilometers.

**The Reach.** On Tallis, the Compact's settler militias have fought the Navy garrison and the Landholders' League since the killings at Dry Creek in 5027u. In 5028u the Compact held the town of Sallow Ford for eleven weeks. CARILLON refused to direct the *Severn*'s guns against it, and Commodore Ferrante took it back with ground artillery instead. Several hundred civilians died.

**Gloam's night side.** Purview troops are clearing the Coldwell Claims' camps from the frozen workings one at a time. The miners fight from the deep levels with frames smuggled in aboard Free Lines landers.

**Pennant.** Corvel landed six hundred hired frames on Pennant's empty southern continent in 5028u to hold its claim. Early this year the Synod's Pilgrim Guard destroyed their dropships, and it has kept them under siege ever since.

**In the lanes.** Gallowglass boarding crews seize Free Lines ships for the Basin Office, and the Cormorant Regulars fight back. Corvel and the Long Families have been killing each other over pirated charts since 5028u. The Basin Office, Smith-Shimano and the Ringwreckers are fighting over Tarn's Absent yards. The Synod and the Purview have exchanged fire over the Orison tide, the Tallymen and the Cormorants fight along the Sable Run, and Halloran's Heirs raid the refugee ships leaving Orison.

**Other trouble.** Custodians have attacked at least fourteen deep extraction sites on Scald and Gloam. On Cairn, Kerr-Varga's militia has turned back a Bench inspection team while the company races to get its mines working before the Bench rules on whether the planet's organisms are sapient. On the Marrow, Compact sympathizers have bombed the Hangar Ring twice since the thaw riots.

## Year twelve

Kasprzak is seventy-eight and has not been seen in public since early summer. The Instrument says nothing about how a Steward-Admiral is to be succeeded. The Long Watch backs Commodore Isaac Ferrante, who commands the Tallis garrison, and the Restorationists back Captain Leyla Aranda-Voss of the *Kestrel Accord*. Both have been meeting with free company commanders, with Director Sato of the Basin Office and with envoys from Gloam. A month ago Saltmarsh Company pulled two hundred frames off the Seam and brought them back to the Marrow without saying why.

Four months ago the gate opened for nine seconds and released a single damaged Union courier drone, the first thing to come through since the Closing. Its encryption matches nothing in the archive. Its internal clock reads 5371u, which would mean more than three centuries have passed on the other side. The Navy took it aboard the *Kestrel Accord* and has said nothing about what it carries.

The Bench has since petitioned that the drone's arrival meets the terms of Article 2 and that the Instrument has therefore lapsed. Command's answer is that a message nobody can read does not count as communication, and the Long Watch has said it will not accept any ruling to the contrary. The Vigil has marched through the Stacks in support of the petition. THRESHOLD has asked for access to the drone and been refused. The drone is still aboard the *Kestrel Accord*, under Aranda-Voss's command.
