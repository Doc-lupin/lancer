# The Basin — Geography

Nov 4, 2026 · @Luna

## The shape of the Basin

The Basin is about fifty light-years across, a crowded knot of more than a hundred stars on the far side of the gate. Spacers divide it into regions. A region is a group of stars, as few as two or as many as a dozen, close enough together that local lanes link them in a matter of hours. Between regions run the primary lanes, which take anywhere from a day to nearly three weeks to cross. The [official map](https://claude.ai/artifact/9DqroZ6uXdHPxxTesEvHZs) shows regions and primary lanes only. There are far too many local lanes to chart, and a good number of them shift from year to year.

In physical space the Basin is a rough sphere. It is the lanes that give it a shape, and on the map that shape is four arms of very different size meeting at a crossroads. Ostrom and Tallis sit at the crossroads, a day apart. The coreward arm climbs from Tallis toward the galactic center and the trailing arm runs out from Tallis against the turn of the galaxy. From Ostrom, the spinward arm reaches the other way and the rimward arm falls away toward the galactic rim. Directions follow Union convention: coreward and rimward toward and away from the center, spinward and trailing with and against its rotation.

Each arm grows stranger the further it runs from the crossroads. Most regions near the middle hold worlds people can live on, or at least work on. The regions at the tips of the arms are frontier: partly surveyed, rich, and in several cases dangerous in ways nobody yet understands. Beyond the frontier the charts simply stop.

## Lanes

Every primary lane in the Basin was built by the Absent, and nobody knows how. A lane is a corridor through which a ship covers in days a distance that would take decades at sublight speed. Ordinary drives can enter one, and from inside, the trip looks like a long dark tunnel lit only by the ship's own running lights. Lane times are given in days from mouth to mouth, and they vary a little with the ship and the season.

Lanes do not all behave alike. Spacers recognize five kinds.

| Lane type | How it behaves                                                                                                                             |
|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Stable    | Holds its course and length year to year, give or take a day.                                                                              |
| Drifting  | Its length and exit point wander over weeks or months. Following one safely takes lattice-tuned navigation and a recent chart.             |
| Tidal     | Opens for about two weeks in every seventy-three days, in step with the pulse of the Lantern stars, and stays closed the rest of the time. |
| One-way   | Runs in one direction only. From the wrong end, the mouth will not take a ship at all.                                                     |
| Silent    | The mouth is charted, but nothing that has gone in has ever come back.                                                                     |

Local lanes, the short hops between stars inside a region, are almost always stable. The few that drift are well known to the people who live near them.

## Lattice

Lattice is a crystalline mineral that stores energy at densities nothing in Union's catalogue approaches. Refined, it stands in for the paracausal components the expedition's printers can no longer make, and lattice-tuned instruments are the only reliable way to hold a course through a drifting lane. Almost every frame, ship and printer in the Basin depends on it, so where it is found matters more than almost anything else about a region.

It is rarely found where people would most like to live. Across the Basin, worlds with good air, water and soil carry little or no lattice, and the richest deposits lie on worlds that are frozen, scorched, starless or otherwise hostile. Nobody has a confirmed explanation. The most widely held theory is that the Absent's climate machinery draws lattice out of the crust of the worlds it regulates, perhaps to run on. That would explain why garden worlds have none, and why small seams are now appearing on Orison as its machinery fails.

Most lattice grows in seams through rock, much as quartz does. On some worlds it forms crusts or nodules near the surface, and in a few places, such as the Furnace and the Mire, it occurs as loose fields or suspended dust in open space, which is harder to gather but needs no mining. Refined lattice also turns up in Absent wreckage, already processed.

Each region entry rates its lattice on a simple scale.

| Rating      | What it means                                               |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| None        | No workable lattice                                         |
| Trace       | Detectable, but not worth mining                            |
| Small       | Enough to supply local printers, not enough to export       |
| Moderate    | A steady export in modest quantities                        |
| Rich        | A major source for the Basin                                |
| Very rich   | Among the largest deposits that can be reached easily       |
| Exceptional | Larger than anything in the inner Basin, and hard to get at |
| Unknown     | Not surveyed well enough to say                             |

The result is that the regions where most people live have almost no lattice of their own. The crossroads and the coreward arm depend almost entirely on lattice brought in from the spinward and rimward arms and from the frontier.

## The crossroads

The two regions at the center of the map are the most travelled in the Basin, and the only pair joined by a single day's lane.

### Ostrom

**Lanes:** Tallis, 1 day · Gloam, 6 days · Scald, 4 days · the Quiet, inbound only, unknown

**Lattice:** Small. Lattice turns up as nodules in Marl's asteroid belt, enough to keep the region's own printers supplied but not enough to export. None has been found on Ostrom's planets or anywhere near the gate.

The Ostrom region is three stars a few hours apart. Ostrom itself is a quiet orange star with six planets, none of them habitable. The gate hangs well beyond the orbit of the outermost, a ring two thousand kilometers across with nothing else near it. The Marrow orbits Ostrom IV, a cold super-Earth with a thin nitrogen atmosphere and nothing on its surface worth landing for. Cinder, nine hours away by local lane, is a burned-out white dwarf with a scatter of airless planets. Marl, the third star, is a red dwarf ringed by a thick asteroid belt, and most of the Marrow's raw material came out of it.

Ostrom has six primary lane mouths. Three lead to Tallis, Gloam and Scald. The other three are the Silent Mouths, clustered a few million kilometers from the gate on its far side. Instruments register them as lane mouths and register nothing beyond them. The space around the gate is otherwise empty, cleaner of dust and debris than anywhere else in the region, as though something keeps it swept.

### Tallis

**Lanes:** Ostrom, 1 day · Pennant, 5 days · Halyard, 3 days

**Lattice:** None. Tallis itself has none at all. Brace's airless worlds carry faint traces, far too thin to be worth working.

Tallis is the largest garden world found in the Basin, mild and wet, with two continents, two small moons and a long chain of river valleys running down the larger continent's eastern side. Most of its people live in those valleys and along the coasts. The interior of the larger continent is a high, dry upland called the Reach, where the rains fail more years than they come. Several kilometers beneath the crust, Absent machinery regulates the planet's air and rainfall. Survey teams found the first of these structures in 5021u and have since mapped eleven, spaced around the planet in a pattern nobody has explained.

The Tallis region includes two other stars. Brace, a red dwarf a few hours trailing, has no habitable worlds but holds the region's lane mouth toward Halyard. Marchwell, coreward, is a dim brown dwarf with almost nothing around it except the mouth of the long lane to Pennant.

## The coreward arm

The coreward arm is the greenest part of the Basin. Its inner regions hold two garden worlds and an ocean world within a few days of each other, and its frontier holds more garden worlds than anywhere else.

### Pennant

**Lanes:** Tallis, 5 days · Orison, 2 days · Wending, 3 days · the Lantern Reach, 9 days

**Lattice:** None. Survey has found nothing workable anywhere in the region, either on Pennant or on the mining worlds around Tew and Ashby.

Pennant is cooler than Tallis and almost entirely forested, with long winters and short, wet summers. It has two continents. The northern one is cut by fjords and broad river valleys and holds nearly all of the planet's settlements. The southern continent is wilder and largely empty, and near its center stands the planet's largest Absent climate structure. From orbit it shows as a ring of bare rock about forty kilometers across, where nothing grows and snow never settles. Pennant's two companion stars, the red dwarfs Tew and Ashby, are a few hours away and hold most of the region's mining.

### Orison

**Lanes:** Pennant, 2 days · Gloam, 5 days, tidal · the Orrery, 11 days, drifting

**Lattice:** Trace, rising. Orison had none when it was first surveyed. Since its machinery began to fail, small seams have appeared in the rock near several of the failing structures, and each new survey finds a little more.

Orison was a garden world when the probes charted it, and in places it still is. Its Absent climate machinery is failing. Winters get longer every year, and the storms that come off its oceans are getting stronger, pushing the sea inland along the coasts in surges that never fully recede. The interior plains, once the planet's best farmland, now freeze for half the year. Survey crews studying the machinery report that parts of it have stopped entirely while others run erratically, as if trying to compensate. Orison's tidal lane mouth toward Gloam lies at the edge of the region and opens only with the Lantern tide.

### Wending

**Lanes:** Pennant, 3 days · the Pale, 8 days · Halyard, 6 days, drifting

**Lattice:** Trace. Small amounts form around deep vents on the seabed, kilometers down, where they are hard to reach and too sparse to matter.

Wending is an ocean world with no continents, only volcanic archipelagos scattered across a single sea. Two large moons raise heavy tides, and the archipelagos flood and drain twice a day. Most of the people there live in floating towns moored in the sheltered lagoons, which ride the tides on long cables. The sea is deep and warm and full of life, much of it unclassified. Wending's sun is a hot white star, and the region's other two stars are dim enough to be seen from the surface only at night.

### The Lantern Reach

**Lanes:** Pennant, 9 days

**Lattice:** Trace. The garden worlds have none. A few of the Reach's airless moons carry thin seams, and that is all.

The Lantern Reach is a chain of nine stars that brighten and dim together on a fixed schedule of seventy-three days. No natural process explains it, and tidal lanes all over the Basin open and close in step with it. Long-range survey has found at least six garden worlds in the Reach, more than in any other region, and two of them have been settled.

Verdance is a garden world whose climate machinery runs hot. Its forests stand two hundred meters tall, its growing season never ends, and its predators are big enough to kill a frame. Two of the three attempts to settle it have been abandoned. Stillwater is an ocean world with no tides and almost no wind, its surface flat as glass for weeks at a time. Absent platforms the size of cities float on it at regular intervals, and one of them has been settled. The other four garden worlds have been seen only from orbit.

### The Pale

**Lanes:** Wending, 8 days · the Furnace, 5 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Moderate. Thin crusts of lattice coat parts of the airless worlds closest to the white dwarfs, as though laid down from outside. They are easy to strip and slow to replace.

The Pale is a region of dead and dying stars: white dwarfs, a neutron star, and the cold remnants of something larger that burned out long before the Absent came. It is dim and cold, and its worlds are airless. For as long as anyone has been listening, a repeating Absent signal has been broadcasting from somewhere inside it, once every forty-one hours. Three survey crews have gone looking for the source, and none has found it.

### The Orrery

**Lanes:** Orison, 11 days, drifting · the Shoals, 6 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Rich, in one place only. The nine worlds hold almost none, but the central structure is veined with it through and through. The Orrery is rich on paper and very hard to mine in practice.

At the center of the Orrery is a single Absent structure, a dark object about the size of a small moon, and around it nine worlds move in orbits far too regular to be natural. There is no star at the center. The worlds are lit faintly by a red dwarf several light-hours away and warmed, somehow, by the structure itself, and their orbits are in exact resonance with one another. Survey crews report that the alignment of the nine worlds is never quite the same from one visit to the next, even though every individual orbit, when measured, appears perfectly stable.

## The trailing arm

The trailing arm is short and tightly packed. Its three inner regions are only a day or two apart, and beyond them the arm opens onto two of the strangest regions in the Basin.

### Halyard

**Lanes:** Tallis, 3 days · Lumen, 1 day · Sable, 2 days · Wending, 6 days, drifting

**Lattice:** None. The region is ice and dim stars, and its value lies in water and reaction mass.

Halyard is a region of four small, dim stars and no habitable worlds. What it has instead is ice. Its outer systems are crowded with comets and ice moons, and it has been the trailing arm's main source of water and reaction mass since the Lean Years. Halyard Station sits in the largest of the four systems, anchored to a shattered ice moon in a wide orbit. The drifting lane to Wending opens near the station, and its drift is fast enough to watch: the exit point moves noticeably from one month to the next.

### Lumen

**Lanes:** Halyard, 1 day · Sable, 1 day · the Furnace, 7 days, drifting

**Lattice:** Small. The metal-rich planets of Lumen's two orange dwarfs carry narrow seams, enough to supply local printers and little more.

Lumen's primary is a brown dwarf, too small to be a true star, that glows a dull red and lights its single large moon in shades of rust. The moon is rocky and tidally heated, warm enough near its volcanic belts for pressurized settlement, and it holds Lumen Station. The region's other stars are a pair of orange dwarfs a few hours away, both with rocky planets rich in metals. On clear nights the Furnace can be seen from Lumen's moon as a smudge of light low on the trailing horizon.

### Sable

**Lanes:** Halyard, 2 days · Lumen, 1 day · the Hollows, 10 days, outbound only · Tarn, 12 days, drifting

**Lattice:** None. Nothing in the region holds workable lattice.

Sable is a dim brown dwarf with a handful of dark moonlets and no worlds worth the name. Its port is built from hulls moored around the largest moonlet. What makes the region matter is its lanes. The lane to the Hollows runs one way, outward only. The lane to Tarn, called the Sable Run, is the longest drifting lane in the inner Basin, and its length has varied between ten and fifteen days in the time it has been charted.

### The Furnace

**Lanes:** Lumen, 7 days, drifting · the Pale, 5 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Rich. Lattice forms in loose fields that drift through the nebula, and it seems to regrow, slowly, where it has been gathered. Collecting it takes no mining at all, only shielding and patience.

The Furnace is a stellar nursery, a glowing nebula full of young, hot stars and worlds still forming out of the dust around them. Its radiation blinds sensors and burns out unshielded electronics within days, and its gravity is uneven enough to make even its local lanes drift. Lattice forms there in the open, in loose drifting fields rather than seams, and can be gathered by any ship willing to take the radiation. Nothing can see far inside the Furnace, which is why some ships go there and most stay away.

### The Hollows

**Lanes:** in from Sable, 10 days, one way · out to Tarn, 6 days, one way · the Mire, 5 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Exceptional. Several of the rogue worlds carry seams under their ice larger than anything in the inner Basin, clustered mostly around the heat engines. Reaching them means cutting through kilometers of ice, and hauling the ore home means two one-way lanes.

The Hollows have no stars at all. They are a scatter of rogue planets drifting in the dark, kept warm by Absent heat engines that nobody has managed to switch off or explain. Their surfaces are black and frozen, but beneath the ice their oceans are liquid, some of them kilometers deep. Several of the Hollows hold lattice in quantities that make Scald look poor, and the heat engines appear to run on it. The local lanes between the rogue worlds are short and stable, so the region is easier to cross than its reputation suggests. Getting in and out is harder, since both of its primary lanes run one way.

## The spinward arm

The spinward arm is the longest and emptiest of the four. Gloam is six days from Ostrom, the longest lane out of the crossroads, and beyond Gloam the arm thins out into the Shoals.

### Gloam

**Lanes:** Ostrom, 6 days · Halvard, 2 days · the Shoals, 4 days, outbound only · Orison, 5 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Rich. Seams run all through the frozen night side, deep and dependable. The cold makes them hard to work, and the deepest of them run down into the ground the custodians defend.

Gloam is tidally locked to a dim red star, with one face in permanent day and the other in permanent night. The day side is a dry, wind-scoured desert under a sun that never moves. The night side is frozen solid, cold enough to stop an unheated reactor, and full of lattice. Between them runs the twilight band, a strip a few hundred kilometers wide where the temperature allows people to live, and that is where Gloam's settlements are. Crews mining deep on the night side, below about four hundred meters, have found Absent machinery that is still awake. The region's only other star is a cool orange dwarf a few hours away, with an asteroid belt and little else.

### Halvard

**Lanes:** Gloam, 2 days · Ferrous, 9 days, drifting · in from the Shoals, 9 days, one way

**Lattice:** Trace. A few of Halvard's moons carry thin seams. The refinery on Halvard's Rest runs mostly on ore brought in from elsewhere in the spinward arm.

Halvard is a gas giant with a dozen moons, orbiting a yellow star with no other planets of note. The largest moon, Halvard's Rest, is an ice world with a thin atmosphere and a deep ocean under its crust, and its lattice refinery processes ore brought in from across the spinward arm. The other moons range from volcanic rock to captured comets. The gas giant itself carries a storm larger than Tallis that has not moved in twelve years of observation. The drifting lane to Ferrous, known as the Halvard Gap, opens close to the planet's ring system.

### The Shoals

**Lanes:** in from Gloam, 4 days, one way · out to Halvard, 9 days, one way · the Orrery, 6 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Rich, and exceptional at Choir. Across the Shoals, lattice lies in shallow seams on small worlds, often close enough to the surface for a single crew to work. Choir's rings are almost pure lattice, the purest large deposit known in the Basin.

The Shoals are dozens of small, closely packed systems, mostly red dwarfs, joined by short local lanes that drift faster than anywhere else in the Basin. A chart of the Shoals is usually out of date within months, and crews who work there keep their own. Lattice is common, often close to the surface. Two worlds are better known than the rest. Choir is a gas giant whose rings are almost pure lattice, and the local lane to it drifts so quickly that nobody has held a stable route for more than a few weeks. Bellows is a cold rocky world whose Absent machinery floods its atmosphere with oxygen for five months of each year and leaves it unbreathable for the other seven.

## The rimward arm

The rimward arm is the Basin's industrial heart and its roughest country. Its inner regions are rich in metal and lattice and hard to live on, and its frontier is the most dangerous on the map.

### Scald

**Lanes:** Ostrom, 4 days · Ferrous, 2 days · Tarn, 3 days · Cairn, 5 days

**Lattice:** Very rich. The seams beneath the glassfields are the largest that can be reached easily anywhere in the Basin, wide, deep and unusually pure. Fine grains of lattice are also scattered through the glass itself.

Scald is hot and dry, with thin air and a sun that burns white. Nearly half its surface was fused to glass in some catastrophe thousands of years before anyone arrived. The glassfields are flat, almost featureless plains that run for thousands of kilometers, cracked into plates and low ridges, with hardly any cover anywhere on them. Beneath the glass lie the richest lattice seams in the inner Basin. The unglassed half of the planet is desert and broken highland. As on Gloam, deep crews on Scald have run into custodians below about four hundred meters. Scald's region includes two smaller stars whose rocky planets show the same glassing in places, as though whatever burned Scald reached them too.

### Ferrous

**Lanes:** Scald, 2 days · the Boneyard, 6 days · Halvard, 9 days, drifting

**Lattice:** Small. Lattice is mixed through some of the asteroid belts in small amounts. Most of Ferrous's wealth is ordinary metal.

Ferrous is a region of four stars with almost no planets and an enormous amount of rock. Its systems are full of metal-rich asteroid belts, the remains of worlds that either never formed or were broken up long ago, and in places the belts are so dense that ships have to thread them by local lane. The Basin's largest foundries are built into the biggest of the asteroids. Ferrous is also the nearest inner region to the Boneyard, and much of what comes out of the Boneyard passes through it.

### Tarn

**Lanes:** Scald, 3 days · the Mire, 7 days, drifting · Sable, 12 days, drifting · in from the Hollows, 6 days, one way

**Lattice:** Moderate. Lattice is locked into the ice of the rings as grains and small lumps that have to be melted out. Tarn's rock has almost none.

Tarn is a cold rocky planet circled by rings broad enough to pick out by telescope from neighboring stars. Inside the rings, hidden by ice and debris, are the ruins of an Absent shipyard: cradles and gantries kilometers long, most of them broken, some still holding the frames of things that were never finished. The yards were found in 5025u, and only a fraction of them has been surveyed. Tarn's region has two other stars, and with four lane mouths it is one of the busiest junctions in the rimward arm.

### Cairn

**Lanes:** Scald, 5 days · out to the Deeps, 11 days, one way

**Lattice:** Moderate. Shallow seams lie under the fields of stone structures, and the organisms seem to gather where the seams are thickest. Nobody has yet mined on Cairn without disturbing the structures nearby.

Cairn sits alone at the end of a five-day lane from Scald, the last region before the rimward frontier. Its main world is cold and cloudy, with a dense atmosphere and an overcast that almost never breaks. Colonial organisms there build stone structures several meters high, some of them in rows and rings that run for kilometers, and the structures appear to respond to nearby lattice. Survey footage shows some of them standing hundreds of meters from where they were recorded a week earlier. The lane onward to the Deeps opens at the edge of the region and runs one way.

### The Deeps

**Lanes:** in from Cairn, 11 days, one way · out to the Boneyard, 18 days, one way · the Mire, 4 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Unknown. The region has not been surveyed well enough to rate, but every long-range reading brought back from it suggests large deposits.

The Deeps are the furthest charted regions in the rimward arm, reached by a one-way lane and left by an eighteen-day run to the Boneyard. Few crews have been there and fewer have stayed long. What is known is that the region holds at least seven stars, several of them unusually old, and Ashlar, the only place the Absent are known to have built above ground. Ashlar is a field of empty stone towers on an airless world, hundreds of them, some more than a kilometer tall. The first crew to land there could not account for nineteen hours of their stay.

### The Boneyard

**Lanes:** Ferrous, 6 days · in from the Deeps, 18 days, one way

**Lattice:** Moderate. Almost all of it is refined lattice built into the Absent wrecks, already processed. Salvaging it means cutting into hulls that are not always dead.

The Boneyard is full of Absent wreckage: megastructures the size of moons, broken open and drifting in loose orbits around three dim stars. Some appear to have been ships and some stations, and some match nothing anyone can name. Salvage crews strip them for alloys nobody can manufacture. Custodians are more common in the Boneyard than anywhere else in the Basin, and some of the wrecks still have power, which shows up on sensors as heat and, now and then, as light.

### The Mire

**Lanes:** Tarn, 7 days, drifting · the Deeps, 4 days, tidal · the Hollows, 5 days, tidal

**Lattice:** Rich. Lattice hangs through the dust cloud as fine grains, enormous in total quantity and slow to collect in useful amounts. The grains are thought to be part of the reason the Mire's local lanes behave so strangely.

The Mire is a dark cloud of dust and gas thick enough to hide the stars inside it. Within the cloud the local lanes branch and rejoin unpredictably, so that two ships entering the same mouth an hour apart can come out in different systems. Some of its stars have never been seen directly and are known only from the lanes that lead to them. Ships go into the Mire to hide, and some come out somewhere they never meant to go.

## Beyond the map

### The Quiet

The Quiet is the name spacers give to whatever lies beyond Ostrom's Silent Mouths. The three mouths are charted, and nothing else about them is. Every probe sent in from Ostrom has disappeared, and nothing has ever come out. The Silent Mouths appear to be tidal, opening and closing on a cycle nobody has worked out, and the best current guess is that they run one way, into Ostrom. Nobody knows how far the Quiet lies from anywhere else, which is why its lane time on the map is marked as unknown.

**Lattice:** Unknown.

### Uncharted space

The official map ends at the frontier, but the lanes do not. Most frontier regions have lane mouths that lead further out, into space nobody has surveyed. Long-range observation suggests the Basin holds well over a hundred stars, and only a fraction of them appear on any chart. More than three hundred lane routes are listed as unconfirmed, and crews find new ones every year.
