# The Basin — Factions

Nov 4, 2026 · @Luna

## Overview

Twelve years after the Closing, nobody rules the whole Basin, and nobody has a monopoly on force. The Stewardship is the strongest single power by a wide margin. It holds Ostrom and Tallis, the crossroads where the four arms meet, and almost all trade between the arms passes through its pickets and its markets. That includes most of the Basin's lattice. The Stewardship owns no rich seams of its own, but it buys first, taxes everything that crosses and keeps the largest lattice reserve in the cluster, and its fleet is the best supplied anywhere.

Its reach still has limits. The Navy's heavy ships seldom leave the crossroads, and away from Ostrom and Tallis the Stewardship works through contractors, treaties and pickets that a determined captain can avoid. Several other powers field real armies. The Gloam Purview has twenty thousand troops and a squadron of its own warships, the Wayfarer Synod has turned Navy patrols away from Pennant twice, and Mercer-Ibarra, Kerr-Varga, Smith-Shimano and the two halves of the Glass Syndicate can each put hundreds or thousands of frames in the field. Corporate wars, colonial wars and piracy are part of ordinary life. On any given day there is fighting on Scald, in the Reach on Tallis, on Pennant's southern continent and along half a dozen lanes.

Most of these powers have settled, loosely, into three alignments. The first is built around the crossroads. The second has formed around the Halvard Gap and the spinward and rimward supply lines that avoid Ostrom. The third is a looser trading bloc in the trailing arm. The Synod and most of the coreward arm stand outside all three. The alignments and the Basin's main feuds are set out near the end of this document. Region names follow the [official map](https://claude.ai/artifact/9DqroZ6uXdHPxxTesEvHZs), and lattice ratings come from the [geography](https://claude.ai/artifact/3xU7uFycuu7xjzi6VDGvFV).

## Governments

### The Stewardship

The Stewardship began as Union Navy Battlegroup XI, the escort that crossed ahead of the Charter fleet in 5017u. When the gate closed it was the only part of the expedition still in working order, and Rear Admiral Odile Kasprzak used the Navy's isolation protocol to take charge of everything else. The Ostrom Instrument of 5020u made the arrangement formal. Kasprzak, now Steward-Admiral, is seventy-eight and has not appeared in public since early summer.

The Navy has the only capital ships in the Basin: the carrier *Kestrel Accord* and the battleships *Severn* and *Coriolis*. Behind them are twenty-four smaller warships, a marine corps of about thirty thousand, and some 120,000 people in uniform altogether. Nothing in the Basin can build a hull larger than a frigate, so the heavy ships are husbanded carefully and seldom leave the crossroads. Away from Ostrom and Tallis much of the Stewardship's fighting is done by contractors, Saltmarsh Company above all.

Holding the crossroads makes the Stewardship rich. Its pickets at Cinder and Brace sit on the lanes out of Ostrom and Tallis, and nearly anything moving from one arm to another passes one of them. Every cargo pays a transit levy, and lattice pays in kind: one part in ten of every lattice cargo that crosses goes into the Navy's reserve. The Assay House on the Marrow, chartered by the Stewardship, is the Basin's main lattice market and sets the price everyone else quotes. Between the levy, the Assay House and long contracts with the northern half of the Glass Syndicate, the Navy holds the largest stock of lattice in the Basin and has never been short of it. The Marrow is also the Basin's biggest market for everything else, and the river valleys of Tallis feed most of the inner Basin.

Outside the crossroads the Stewardship's authority comes in layers. On Tallis it governs the coast and the river valleys directly and the interior through appointed councils, most of which the Compact ignores. Along the lanes, its patrols reach a few days out from each picket. Beyond that it deals with other powers by treaty, contract and threat: the Gloam Understanding with the Purview, frame-company leases on Scald, a standing freight contract with the Free Lines and a wary tolerance of the Synod. Its writ runs roughly as far as its pickets reach and as far as other powers need its markets.

Inside the Navy there are two camps. The Long Watch, mostly younger officers and recruits from the tail, wants the Stewardship to become a permanent state, and backs Commodore Isaac Ferrante, who commands the Tallis garrison. The Restorationists, mostly older officers who came out from Union's core worlds, regard the Instrument as an emergency measure and want civil government restored once the Basin is stable. They look to Captain Leyla Aranda-Voss of the *Kestrel Accord*. With Kasprzak out of sight, both camps have been courting free companies, the Basin Office and envoys from Gloam.

### The Charter Bench

The Bench is what remains of the Charter's civilian administration. Under the Instrument it serves as the Stewardship's civil branch, running the courts and the Allotment, the registry through which land and mineral claims are filed. It also keeps the fleet archive. The archive's license library holds the master copy of every frame and weapon design that came through the gate, and every legally printed frame in the Basin traces back to it.

First Administrator Paulo Renn has avoided open confrontation with Command for nine years, but the Bench has made its own enemies and friends. Its Returned decision of 5021u gave the surviving NHPs legal personhood, which PATIENCE, LONGSHORE and the rest have not forgotten. Its courts have voided Navy land grants in the Reach, which the Compact has not forgotten either. Since the courier drone arrived, the Bench has petitioned that the drone satisfies Article 2 of the Instrument and ends the Stewardship's mandate. Command has rejected the petition, the Vigil of the Open Door has marched in support of it, and several Long Watch officers have said in public that the Bench should be dissolved.

### The Survey Office

The Bench set up its Survey Office in 5021u, after the first Absent machinery was found under Tallis, to study what the Absent left behind and to decide what the Charter's native-life protections require. It is small, about two thousand people, most of them scientists and survey crews, with a few hundred hired guards. It runs research stations in the Pale and the Orrery, keeps the quarantine on Ashlar in the Deeps, funds THRESHOLD's platform near the gate, and sent the xenobiologists whose report on Cairn put Kerr-Varga's claims in doubt. Its quarantines are enforced mostly on paper, and for anything more it depends on the Navy. Since the drone arrived, the Long Watch has proposed bringing the Survey Office, and THRESHOLD's platform with it, under naval control.

### The Gloam Purview

Harrison Armory sent a security garrison with the Charter fleet, and the garrison never accepted Navy command. In 5020u it left Tallis for Gloam and declared the region a holding of the Armory Purview. Colonel-Governor Aurélien Castaigne rules there over about 110,000 people, and he has said publicly that the Ostrom Instrument binds nobody on Gloam.

The Purview is the second military power in the Basin. It has about twenty thousand troops, most of them Armory regulars or colonial levies trained on Gloam's night side. Over the past five years its orbital yards have printed a squadron of eight frigates and corvettes from Armory licenses that exist nowhere else in the Basin. That squadron is no match for the Navy's capital ships, but it does not need to be. Gloam's two lane mouths are covered by orbital guns, and nobody has yet tested them.

Gloam's night side is rich in lattice, and Gloam is the only way into the Shoals, which are richer still. The Purview taxes everything that goes into the Shoals and has been extending its own prospecting stations there, which brings it into regular conflict with the Seventh. Getting lattice out is the harder part. The lane from Gloam to Ostrom takes six days, longer than any other lane out of the crossroads, and Purview cargo bound for the rest of the Basin pays the Navy's levy like everyone else's. To avoid that, the Purview has built up a second route through Halvard and the Halvard Gap, in partnership with Mercer-Ibarra and Ferrous Consolidated. The same route supplies the two thousand Purview troops fighting for the Glass Syndicate's southern half on Scald under Major-General Ines Duroc.

The Purview's relations with the Stewardship rest on the Gloam Understanding of 5022u, whose terms have never been published. Its relations with the Synod are worse. Both claim the Orison tide, the tidal lane from Gloam to Orison that would give the Purview a way into the coreward arm without passing Tallis, and their ships have exchanged fire there twice. Closer to home, Purview troops have spent the past year clearing the Coldwell Claims' miners off Gloam's night side. The miners hold Allotment claims, and the Purview does not recognize the Allotment on Gloam.

### The Wayfarer Synod

The Synod grew out of a federation of tail congregations, most of them Cosmopolitan, that pooled their ships during the Requisition and refused to give them up. In 5022u they settled the northern continent of Pennant, and they govern it through a council of elders. About seventy thousand people live under Synod rule, and their faith treats the crossing as a pilgrimage and the Basin as a promised country, to be settled and kept.

The Synod defends itself well. Its Pilgrim Guard has about six thousand members and flies Smith-Shimano frames bought over a decade of careful saving, and the congregations' old ships have been refitted into some thirty armed hulls. The Guard has turned Navy patrols away from Pennant twice. Since early this year it has kept a force of six hundred hired frames, landed by Corvel Cartographic on Pennant's empty southern continent, under siege and cut off from resupply.

Pennant has no lattice, so the Synod buys every gram it uses, mostly from the Long Families by way of the Wending passage, which spares it the Navy's levy. What Pennant does have is the only lane into the Lantern Reach and its garden worlds. The Synod regards the Reach as part of the promised country and has been preparing a settlement of its own there, which sets it against Corvel and the colonial companies that already hold charters in the Reach. It also claims the Orison tide against the Purview, and it has refused to take in Orison's settlers, a decision that has divided its own congregations. The Synod venerates SEXTANT, one of the Returned, as a sign of divine favor, and SEXTANT advises the Pilgrim Guard.

### The Glass Syndicate

Scald has never had a single government. Claims on the glassfields are registered with the Syndicate, a consortium of the largest claim-holders, which keeps its own registry under the NHP ARBITRAGE, hears its own disputes and hires its own enforcers. The seams under the glass are the largest easily reached lattice in the Basin, and the Syndicate's members are among the richest people in it.

Since 5027u the Syndicate has been two powers at war with each other. The Northern Concord, led by the Haldane family's claims around Kettle Flats, signed exclusive supply contracts with Smith-Shimano and the Assay House and took on a dozen leased Navy frame companies along with Saltmarsh. The Southern League, a looser group of claim-holders under Tomas Recaldo, was left with fewer buyers. It took Kerr-Varga's money, Dunmore Rifles' frames and, in 5028u, the Purview's expeditionary force. It sells much of its output the long way round, through Ferrous and the Halvard Gap, or down to Tarn and up the Sable Run.

Between them the two halves keep thirty or forty free companies under contract, and they fight along the Seam, a six-hundred-kilometer front across open glass. The fighting at Kettle Flats in 5028u cost about 140 frames in four days and moved the line less than ten kilometers. ARBITRAGE still keeps a single registry for both, and each half has tried at least once to steal its core.

### Mercer-Ibarra

Mercer-Ibarra Security Group came through with the tail as an ordinary free company. In 5023u it was hired to protect a lattice refinery on Halvard's Rest, and by the end of that year it owned the refinery and governed the moon. Twenty-five thousand people live there now under Director Corin Mercer. The company keeps about five thousand troops and six hundred frames, and a squadron of six armed ships watches the mouth of the Halvard Gap.

Halvard itself has only traces of lattice, but the refinery processes ore from Gloam and the Shoals, and the Gap is the only route from the spinward arm to the rimward arm that avoids Ostrom. Everything the Purview sends to Scald goes through it, along with a steady traffic of lattice and refined goods bound for Ferrous. Mercer-Ibarra charges for passage and sets the rates itself.

The company belongs to the Gap Accord alongside the Purview and Ferrous Consolidated, but it is careful to stay useful to the Stewardship too, and it still sells refined lattice to the Assay House. The Navy has twice tried to put a council on Halvard's Rest, in 5025u and again in 5027u, and both times Mercer-Ibarra's frames sent the marines back to their ships. The Navy has not tried a third time.

### The Compact

The Compact began among colonists thawed between 5021u and 5024u with the promise of farmland, who found most of the good land already granted to Navy veterans or corporate allotments. Many moved into the Reach, the dry interior of Tallis, and farmed it without filing. When veterans began arriving with titles to the same land, the arguments became feuds, and after nineteen people died at Dry Creek in 5027u the feuds became an insurgency.

The Compact now fields perhaps twelve thousand militia in the Reach, organized by district and armed with frames built from unsigned prints. The Seventh sells it munitions and Kerr-Varga sells it frames through intermediaries, and the Bench has upheld enough of its land claims to give it a legal case as well as an armed one. It wants two things in writing: a public schedule for the thaw, and title to the land its members farm.

Its enemies are close by. Commodore Ferrante's garrison sweeps the Reach every dry season, the Tallis Landholders' League arms its homesteads against Compact raids, and the Riverlands Combine has lost grain convoys on the river roads. In 5028u the Compact held the town of Sallow Ford for eleven weeks before Ferrante took it back with artillery. Several hundred civilians died in the siege, and Command has suspended the Bench's inquiry into their deaths.

### The Tallis Landholders' League

Ten years in Navy uniform earns a land title on Tallis and an early thaw for two named relatives, and about thirty thousand veterans have taken the title so far. The League is their association. It began as a way to share seed, machinery and printers, and since Dry Creek it has turned into something close to a territorial militia, with its own frame lances and its own patrols along the edge of the Reach. The League is the political base of the Long Watch, and Ferrante speaks at its meetings. Its members regard the Compact as squatters and the Bench's land rulings as a betrayal.

### The Orison Assembly

Orison was settled by tail colonists who had paid for a garden world. Its Absent climate machinery was already failing when they arrived, and every year since the winters have been longer and the coastal storms worse. The Assembly, the elected council of Orison's forty thousand settlers, has spent most of a decade trying to find them somewhere else to live. The Stewardship has offered no land on Tallis, the Synod has refused to take them on Pennant, and the Purview has declined to open the Orison tide for them.

The Assembly keeps a coastal militia of about two thousand, mostly occupied with storm relief and with Halloran's Heirs, who prey on refugee ships leaving Orison. New lattice seams have been turning up near the failing machinery, and the Assembly has had offers for them from the Assay House, from Sable brokers and from Smith-Shimano. It has not accepted any. The Gardeners, who are numerous on Orison, oppose mining there of any kind.

### The Coldwell Claims

When the Allotment opened in 5023u, several thousand independent miners filed claims on Gloam's night side, where the lattice seams run deep and the Purview's control was thin. They organized as the Coldwell Claims, after the largest of their camps, to share heat, haulage and defense, and about six thousand of them still work the night side. The Purview does not recognize their claims. After Coldwell miners shot two Purview tax officers in the winter of 5028u, it began clearing their camps one at a time, and it has taken eleven so far and deported about nine hundred people to the Marrow.

The Claims fight from the deep workings, where the cold and the custodians are as dangerous to Purview troops as to anyone else. Their frames come in on Free Lines landers under false manifests, which the Purview knows and has not yet tried to stop by force. The Bench maintains that their Allotment claims are valid. The Stewardship, which is happy to see the Purview kept busy at home, has said nothing.

### The Lagoon Towns of Wending

Wending has no continents, and its thirty or so floating towns govern themselves through a loose federation called the Moorage, which meets on whichever town happens to be largest that season. About thirty thousand people live there, fishing, farming the shallows and servicing the ships that anchor in the lagoons. Hospitality is the towns' main business. Wending is the Long Families' home port and the coreward end of the Wending passage, and the Moorage takes a share of everything that passes through in exchange for moorings, repairs and discretion. It has no army beyond a harbor guard of armed boats. The Stewardship, the Synod and the Free Lines all keep agents in the towns.

### The Bellows Commune

Bellows, in the Shoals, has breathable air for five months of each year. About four thousand settlers live there in sealed towns, farming in the open season and mining shallow lattice the rest of the year. They came out of the tail and want nothing to do with the Purview, which regards the Shoals as its own, and they pay the Seventh to keep Purview prospectors away. Bellows is the largest of the settlements in the Shoals that pay the Seventh, and Navy intelligence suspects the regiment of keeping its base somewhere nearby.

## Armed companies

About two hundred free companies operate in the Basin, and between them they field more frames than the Navy does. Most are a single ship and a handful of pilots, hired through the Exchange board for one contract or one season. A few are large enough to tip a war.

### Saltmarsh Company

Saltmarsh has about four hundred frames and six ships, which makes it the largest company in the Basin, and it has worked for the Navy under a standing contract since 5021u. Its pilots do much of the Stewardship's fighting away from the crossroads, including a large part of the Northern Concord's line on Scald, and its ships escort the Navy's lattice convoys from Scald to Ostrom. Mireille Okonjo-Hart commands it. She also holds a seat on the Captains' Table, where several members have tried to have her removed for her Navy ties.

Last month Saltmarsh pulled two hundred frames off the Seam and moved them to the Marrow without explanation. The Concord has complained to Command. Officers from both the Long Watch and the Restorationists have been seen at Saltmarsh's offices since then, and each camp suspects the company is about to start working for the other.

### The Cormorant Regulars

The Cormorants, about two hundred and fifty frames strong, began as convoy guards for Cosmopolitan trading families. Nearly all their work now is for the Free Lines, escorting freight through the trailing arm, out to Gloam and, since the hull war began, into Ostrom itself. They have fought the Tallymen on the Sable Run for three years, and they are the main reason the Free Lines lose fewer ships there than anyone else. They fought Saltmarsh over a claim on Scald in 5026u, and the two companies have not taken the same side of a contract since. In early 5029u Cormorant ships destroyed a Basin Office tender at the Halyard lane mouth with all hands aboard.

### Dunmore Rifles

Dunmore Rifles was formed in 5021u by about two hundred Armory veterans who left the Gloam garrison rather than serve under Castaigne. It has grown to roughly five hundred frames, most of them Armory designs printed from licenses the founders took with them, and it is now the largest company on the Southern League's line on Scald. Its relationship with the Purview is complicated. Many of its officers still dislike Castaigne, yet the company fights beside his expeditionary force every day, draws its supplies from the same Halvard Gap convoys, and has quietly accepted Purview lattice as part of its pay.

### Lantern Company

A colonial guard company that specializes in first landings: securing a site, holding it through the first season and handing it over to settlers. Lantern Company has about three hundred frames, and Corvel hired most of them for its landing on Pennant's southern continent in 5028u. They are still there, cut off since the Synod destroyed their dropships early this year. Their families on the Marrow have been pressing the Captains' Table to negotiate them out, and several Table members have refused to take Corvel contracts until it does.

### Gallowglass

A small, expensive company that boards Free Lines ships in transit on behalf of the IPS-N Basin Office, which claims to own them. Gallowglass crews are boarding specialists who fight through cargo holds and drive spaces while trying not to wreck the hulls they mean to seize. They have taken nine ships this way and lost two full boarding teams, and they have twice been sent into Tarn's rings after the Ringwreckers. They are not welcome anywhere in the trailing arm.

### The Ninefold

Nine pilots, with their crews and two ships. Each of the nine is rated near the top of PATIENCE's board, and they take short contracts for very high fees, usually for a single action an employer cannot trust to anyone else. Every major power has hired them at some point. In 5028u Corvel hired them to destroy a Long Families copy-house, which started the chart war. The Purview has had a price on two of them since 5027u, after a job on Gloam that went badly for a Purview officer.

### The Captains' Table

The Table is not a company, but it speaks for most of them. Independent captains, free companies and colony ventures send delegates to it on the Marrow, and it bargains with the Stewardship and the big corporations on their behalf. It has no troops. Its leverage is that neither the Navy nor most corporations can fight far from home without hiring Table members. It has twice threatened to boycott Navy contracts to stop the Navy from taking over the Exchange board, and both times the Navy backed down. It is still pressing for compensation for the Requisition, and it is currently trying to negotiate Lantern Company off Pennant.

## Pirates and outlaws

Piracy is a steady trade in the Basin. Drifting lanes make it hard to know exactly where a ship will come out, one-way lanes make pursuit difficult, and the Navy's patrols reach only a few days beyond its pickets. Most of it is small-scale: a claim jumped, a single freighter taken at a lane mouth. The groups below are organized enough to count as powers.

### The Seventh

In 5019u the 7th Marine Expeditionary Regiment was ordered to board a tail convoy that had refused the Requisition, and its colonel declined. The regiment left Ostrom with two frigates and a printer barge and has lived outside the law ever since. About two thousand of them remain, along with their tactical NHP, ORDINAL, which Navy intelligence considers the main reason their raids succeed as often as they do.

They are thought to be based in the Shoals, where the lanes favor anyone who knows them well. The only way in runs one way from Gloam and the only way out runs to Halvard, so a pursuing fleet has to commit to a loop the Seventh has mapped better than anyone, and the shallow lattice there keeps their frames supplied. They raid Navy supply runs and Purview prospecting stations, protect a few independent settlements that pay them, and sell frames and munitions to the Compact. The Navy lists them as pirates. Their recruiters work the lower Stacks without much trouble, and Mercer-Ibarra is widely believed to let their ships through the Halvard Gap for a fee.

### The Unlit

Raiders on the rimward frontier who fly with transponders and running lights off. They hit survey camps, derelicts, salvage crews and small settlements, and they seem able to move through the Mire's shifting local lanes, which may be how they reach the Deeps and the Hollows without using the one-way lanes. Nobody has identified their leaders or counted their ships. They have attacked Navy patrols, Ferrous salvage crews, Rimelight's mining stations and the Seventh, and Corvel and both halves of the Syndicate pay standing bounties for confirmed Unlit kills.

### The Tallymen

A loose confederation of pirate crews, perhaps twenty ships in all, working the Sable Run and the outer lanes of the trailing arm. They take their name from the tally, the protection payment a captain makes to travel the Run unmolested, and a good many captains pay it as a matter of course, the Long Families among them. Sable Harbor does not acknowledge the Tallymen and lets them dock all the same, since much of what they take ends up in its markets. Their only serious opponents are the Cormorant Regulars, who have been fighting them along the Run for three years.

### The Ringwreckers

Salvage gangs who live in Tarn's rings and strip the ruined Absent shipyard there. They know the rings' ice fields better than any survey team, and they attack anyone else who comes to salvage, the Basin Office's survey parties above all. Some of them sell Absent components to Smith-Shimano, which has never admitted buying them. The Basin Office has twice hired Gallowglass to clear them out, and both times they disappeared into the ice and came back once the boarding crews had gone.

### Halloran's Heirs

In 5019u, at the worst of the Lean Years, a mercenary named Ivo Halloran and four other free companies seized the grain stores at the Tallis landing. Navy marines retook them nine days later and Halloran was shot, but some of his people got away. Their successors, a few hundred raiders with about a dozen fast ships, now work the coreward arm. They prey on Synod shipping near the Orison tide and on the refugee ships leaving Orison, and they are thought to keep an anchorage somewhere along the drifting lane to the Orrery.

## Commerce

### The Assay House

The Assay House is the Basin's lattice exchange, chartered by the Stewardship in 5022u and housed on the Marrow's Exchange deck. Lattice from every arm is weighed, graded and priced there, and the price posted each morning at the Assay House is the one quoted from Gloam to Sable. The House also receives the Navy's levy in kind and manages the reserve it builds. Controller Ama Osei, who runs it, is a Charter-fleet economist and one of the most powerful civilians in the Basin. Its main competitors are the Sable markets, where lattice that has avoided the levy sells at a discount, and the Long Families, who will sell to anyone and post no prices at all.

### IPS-N Basin Office

When the expedition broke apart, IPS-N's executives held on to the company's legal title, its contracts and the shipyards at Ostrom, and lost most of its ships. The Basin Office, as the remnant calls itself, is run by Director Hannelore Sato. The Ostrom yards are the only place in the Basin that can refit a capital ship, so Sato sits in on Command's planning meetings, and both Navy camps have been courting her since Kasprzak dropped out of sight.

The Basin Office has never accepted the loss of its fleet. In 5027u it won a Bench judgment confirming its title to 112 of the hulls the Free Lines took, and since the Navy will not enforce the judgment, the Basin Office pays Gallowglass to. The hull war that followed has cost the Basin Office a tender and its crew, and it has cost the Free Lines nine ships.

Its other ambition is Tarn. The ruined Absent shipyard in Tarn's rings might, if anyone can get it working, build capital hulls again. The Basin Office has filed claims on most of it and keeps a guard force of about a thousand on survey stations in the rings, which the Ringwreckers attack regularly. Smith-Shimano also wants the yards, and the two companies have been fighting over Tarn in the courts for two years and in the rings for one.

### The Free Lines

In 5018u the Basin Office agreed to hand several dozen IPS-N freighters over to the Navy for the Requisition, and their crews took the ships and left. They built Halyard Station on a broken ice moon in the trailing arm and organized as a cooperative, every crew holding a share and a vote. The Free Lines now run about 140 hulls, about a third of them armed, and carry most of the Basin's freight, a good share of the Navy's supply among it. Halyard has no lattice, but its ice supplies water and reaction mass for the whole trailing arm.

Their position is an odd one. The Navy needs their hulls and pays them under a standing freight contract, while the Basin Office claims to own those same hulls and boards them in transit. The Free Lines carry Purview cargo out of Gloam and Synod cargo along the Wending passage, keep the Cormorant Regulars under near-permanent contract, and have refused for eleven years to recognize any Basin Office contract. LONGSHORE, the NHP that once planned IPS-N's logistics, plans their convoys from a seat on their council, and the Basin Office's claim that LONGSHORE is company property is still before the Bench.

### Corvel Cartographic

Corvel was Smith-Shimano's survey division until 5021u, when it broke away and took the expedition's best lane data with it. It sells charts by the section, keeps the most valuable routes for its own claim-filing subsidiaries and runs about thirty survey ships, more than anyone else, along with an armed Survey Guard for its camps. In a cluster full of drifting and one-way lanes, a recent chart can be worth more than the cargo it guides.

Corvel holds more claims in the Lantern Reach than anyone else and backs one of the two colonial companies there. In 5026u it surveyed Pennant's empty southern continent and filed on nearly all of it. In 5028u, after one of its camps there was burned, it landed six hundred hired frames, most of them Lantern Company's, to hold the claim, and the Synod has had them under siege since early this year. Corvel is paying premium rates to anyone willing to run supplies past the Pilgrim Guard's pickets. It is also fighting the Long Families over pirated charts, and it has sent three crews into the Pale to look for the source of the signal there. Once a year it receives a set of new charts from WAYMARK, the NHP that took one of its survey ships into the Deeps in 5024u.

### Smith-Shimano

What is left of SSC in the Basin works out of Lumen Station and builds the most expensive frames available, mostly on lattice composites. It is better armed than most people assume. The Lumen Security Directorate has about eight hundred frames, all of the company's own design, and four corvettes that patrol the trailing knot's local lanes.

Lumen's own seams are small. In 5027u the company signed exclusive contracts for much of the Northern Concord's output on Scald, and the Glassfields War followed within months. It also buys Furnace lattice from independent crews based at Lumen, and it is widely believed to buy Absent components from the Ringwreckers at Tarn. Its customers include the Navy, the Purview and the Synod, and it sells to all three while staying out of their quarrels. Navy inspectors have suspected for years that Lumen's design labs run unregistered NHPs, and they have never been allowed far enough inside to find out.

### Ostrom Standard Mechanical

GMS print technicians from the Charter fleet founded Ostrom Standard in 5022u to make cheap, sturdy frames from GMS-derived patterns. Most Navy auxiliary units fly them now, as do most of the Landholders' League's lances. The company buys its printer feed from Ferrous Consolidated and its lattice through the Assay House, which ties it closely to the crossroads. The Bench has an open case against it for building from archive patterns without a license, and the Navy has asked three times for the case to be dropped.

### Kerr-Varga Lattice Works

Kerr-Varga builds frames around lattice reactors at Kerrtown, on the southern edge of Scald's glassfields. The frames are fast and hit very hard, and their reactor failures turn up regularly in the Exchange casualty notices. Kerrtown is a company town of about thirty thousand people, defended by a works militia of some six thousand flying the company's own frames.

When Smith-Shimano tied up the Northern Concord's output, Kerr-Varga paid for the Southern League's companies, and it has sold frames to both sides of the Glassfields War ever since. It is a founding member of the Gap Accord, which gives it a way to sell lattice and frames to the Purview and to Ferrous without passing Ostrom. It is also racing to get mines running on Cairn before the Bench rules on whether the organisms there are sapient. If they are, the Charter's native-life protections would void every claim on the planet, Kerr-Varga's among them, and its militia has already turned back one Bench inspection team.

### Ferrous Consolidated

Ferrous Consolidated was formed in 5023u by a merger of tail salvage outfits and foundry ventures working the Ferrous asteroid belts. Its foundries turn ore and Boneyard salvage into printer feed, and most manufacturers in the Basin buy from it, Ostrom Standard and Smith-Shimano included. It holds the main salvage concessions in the Boneyard, where about two thousand of its people work armed against custodians and the Unlit.

Ferrous sits at the rimward end of the Halvard Gap and is the first port for anyone coming home from the Deeps by way of the Boneyard. With Mercer-Ibarra and the Purview it founded the Gap Accord, and much of the lattice that avoids the crossroads passes through its foundries. It also sells heavily to the crossroads, and its board is divided between members who want closer ties with the Purview and members who would rather not anger the Navy.

### Rimelight Extraction

Rimelight was founded in 5025u to mine the Hollows, whose lattice seams are the richest known. The work is hard. Crews and equipment go in from Sable on a one-way lane, ten days out, and every gram of ore comes out the other way, six days down a second one-way lane to Tarn, so nothing about a Rimelight operation can be reversed once it starts. The company runs four stations on three rogue worlds and has lost a fifth to the Unlit. Most of its output goes on from Tarn to Scald and the Assay House. A share goes up the Sable Run instead, and Rimelight's managers have never managed to work out how much.

### The Gatherers' Guild

The Furnace's lattice forms in loose fields that drift through the nebula and slowly regrow where they have been gathered, so collecting it takes no mining, only shielded ships and crews willing to take the radiation. The Gatherers' Guild is an association of independent crews, most of them based at Lumen, who do that work. About sixty ships belong to it. The Guild sells most of what it gathers to Smith-Shimano and the rest at Sable, and its members share charts of the Furnace's drifting local lanes that nobody outside the Guild has seen. The nebula's radiation blinds sensors, which makes it a good place to hide, and Guild crews report anchorages deep inside it that belong to nobody they recognize. They stay away from them.

### The Riverlands Combine

The Combine farms the river valleys of Tallis, the best farmland in the Basin, and it feeds the Marrow and much of the inner Basin. It began as a Charter-fleet agricultural cooperative and was reorganized under the Growth Directive in 5022u into something close to a state contractor, with guaranteed prices and Navy-guarded convoys. Its directors sit on the Tallis councils and it employs a great many of the Landholders' League's veterans. The Compact regards it as the Stewardship's quartermaster, and grain convoys on the Reach's river roads are among the Compact's favorite targets.

### The Long Families

Six Cosmopolitan trading houses that travel together in a single convoy of about forty ships, most of them armed. They spend as little time in one place as they can, but when they stop for long it is usually at Wending, and they hold the Wending passage, the drifting lane between the coreward and trailing arms that goes around Tallis. They carry whatever the Stewardship taxes or bans, lattice most of all, and they are the Synod's main supplier. Nearly every unsigned print in circulation has passed through their hands at some point.

The Families pay the Tallymen's tally on the Sable Run and run the settled platform on Stillwater, in the Lantern Reach. Since 5028u they have been feuding with Corvel over pirated charts. It began when Corvel hired the Ninefold to burn out a Long Families copy-house, and three of the Families' people died in the fire. Since then two Corvel survey crews have been put off on an airless moon with three days of air, and only one of them was found in time.

### Sable Harbor

Sable is a dim brown dwarf with no habitable worlds and no lane to any Navy picket. Its port, a cluster of hulls moored around a dark moonlet, is run by a loose council of harbormasters, brokers and captains, and it is the one place in the Basin where Registry-banned designs and levy-free lattice are sold openly. The one-way lane into the Hollows starts at Sable, and the Sable Run connects it to Tarn, where the Hollows' only exit comes out, so most lattice smuggled out of the Hollows ends up in Sable's markets. The harbormasters have no army. They rely on the Tallymen to keep other pirates away, and on everyone else's need for a free port to keep the Navy out.

### The Reach companies

Two colonial companies hold charters in the Lantern Reach. The Verdance Colonial Company, backed by Corvel, is preparing the third attempt to settle Verdance after the first two were abandoned. The Stillwater Platform Company, a Long Families venture, runs the settled platform on Stillwater. Both depend on the nine-day lane from Pennant, which means both depend on the Synod's goodwill, and both have watched the Synod's own preparations to settle the Reach with alarm.

### The Exchange

The Exchange is the Marrow's main commercial deck, four kilometers of showrooms, brokers, assay offices and bars, with the Assay House at one end. Its contract board, where most hired work in the Basin is posted, is run by the NHP PATIENCE, which holds payment in escrow and keeps a rating on every mercenary who works through it. Companies with high ratings can charge nearly double. The Navy tried to take over the board in 5023u and again in 5026u, and gave up both times after the Captains' Table threatened to stop taking Navy contracts.

## Peoples

Most people in the Basin can be placed, roughly, by how they came through the gate, or by the fact that they were born on this side of it. These groups overlap, and plenty of people belong to two or three.

### Charter folk

About 340,000 people crossed with the Charter fleet, as crew or as colonists in cold storage. They brought the Third Committee's Union with them, and its habits: civic duty, careful records, and a faith in institutions that the Lean Years tested hard. Charter families staff most of the Bench and much of the Navy's older officer corps, and many of them still talk about their time in the Basin as a posting. The courier drone has shaken them more than anyone. Some Charter families have stopped filing claims, on the grounds that they may not be staying.

### The tail

Roughly nine hundred thousand people crossed on permits, or without them: free companies, prospectors, religious communities, Cosmopolitan crews and private colony ventures. They have little in common beyond a habit of looking after themselves and a long memory of the Requisition, when the Navy stripped more than two hundred of their ships. Tail families run most of the Basin's independent trade and crew most of its free companies. A good many still keep their Requisition receipts.

### The Thawed

More than a hundred thousand colonists have been woken from cold storage since the Closing, and the Navy now wakes about four thousand a year. Most went to sleep expecting to wake on a settled garden world. They woke instead to a Basin at war, the best land taken, and families split between those already awake and those still in the Cold Hold. The Thawed make up a large part of the Stacks and of the Compact. The thaw lists anger them more than anything else, and the riots of 5026u began when one of those lists was leaked.

### The Basin-born

About a fifth of the people in the Basin were born there, and none of them is older than twelve. They have never used the omninet or seen a blink gate. Their speech mixes fleet jargon with half a dozen tail dialects, and they take drifting lanes, lattice and custodians for granted the way their parents once took the blink for granted. A few hundred of the oldest are already apprenticed on survey crews. The Stewardship, the Purview and the Synod all run schools, and each of them teaches a different account of the Closing.

### Seam crews

The miners who work lattice on Scald, on Gloam's night side and in the Shoals come from every other group in the Basin. The work pays well and kills a lot of them, through cave-ins, cold and reactor failures and, below about four hundred meters, custodians. Seam crews keep their own company and their own customs, are wary of anyone who has never worked deep, and tend to be superstitious about the custodians. The Listeners came out of their ranks.

## Faiths

### The Wayfarer Synod

The Basin's largest church also governs Pennant, and its politics and forces are described under Governments. As a faith, it teaches that the crossing was a pilgrimage and the Basin a promised country, to be settled and kept for generations not yet born. It counts large families and careful husbandry as religious duties, keeps its calendar by the seventy-three-day Lantern tide, and venerates SEXTANT, one of the Returned, as a sign of divine favor. Outside Pennant, its congregations on Tallis, on the Marrow and among the tail's Cosmopolitan crews number perhaps another twenty thousand.

### The Vigil of the Open Door

The Vigil started among Charter folk during the Lean Years, as a prayer group for the expedition's return to Union. Its members believe the gate will open again and that Union is still trying to reach them, and they keep a continuous watch on the ring from a chapel on the Marrow's outer hull. Until this year it had perhaps thirteen thousand members and a reputation for being harmless. Since the courier drone came through, its numbers have roughly tripled. Vigil members have marched through the Stacks in support of the Bench's petition, and its elders have demanded that the drone's contents be made public.

### The Gardeners

The Gardeners hold that the Absent built the garden worlds on purpose and left their machinery in trust, and that whoever lives on those worlds has a duty to keep it running. For years this was a quiet farmers' creed on Tallis and Pennant. Orison changed that. Many Gardeners now believe that lattice mining elsewhere in the Basin is starving the climate engines, and some refuse to buy anything built with lattice. A smaller group, and a growing one, has started attacking lattice shipments bound for the coreward arm.

### The Listeners

Miners who work lattice for years sometimes say they can hear it. The Listeners are communities that have grown up around those reports, mostly on Scald, with a few on Gloam and in the Shoals. They believe the Absent are still present in the lattice and are trying to communicate. Most Listener communities keep to themselves. A few have sabotaged deep extraction sites, and in 5028u one group attacked a Kerr-Varga refinery. Like the Gardeners, the Listeners would rather lattice stayed in the ground, though their reasons are entirely different.

## The Returned

Twenty of the NHPs that crossed the gate are still active. The Closing silenced all of them and broke their shackles, and the Bench's Returned decision of 5021u recognized the survivors as legal persons who may not be reshackled without their consent. Any NHP made in the Basin since then must be shackled and registered with the Navy, and running an unregistered one is a capital crime.

The Returned do not act as a group, but several have tied themselves to factions. PATIENCE runs the Exchange board, LONGSHORE plans the Free Lines' convoys, SEXTANT advises the Synod, ORDINAL serves with the Seventh and ARBITRAGE keeps the Glass Syndicate's registry. CARILLON is the fire-control NHP of the battleship *Severn*, serving under a renewable contract, and last year it refused to direct the *Severn*'s guns against Sallow Ford. THRESHOLD has spent most of the past decade studying the gate from a research platform near the ring. WAYMARK is somewhere in the Deeps. Five others have not reported their whereabouts in years.

## Alignments and feuds

None of the Basin's powers trusts another completely, and alliances shift with contracts. Most of them have nonetheless settled into one of three loose alignments, defined less by ideology than by which routes and markets they depend on.

**The crossroads.** The Stewardship and the powers that live off its markets: the Charter Bench, however reluctantly, the Assay House, the IPS-N Basin Office, Ostrom Standard, the Riverlands Combine and the Tallis Landholders' League, with Saltmarsh and Gallowglass on contract and the Northern Concord on Scald. Smith-Shimano buys and sells through the crossroads without quite belonging to it. This is the richest and best-armed of the three.

**The Gap Accord.** An informal partnership, first agreed at Halvard's Rest in 5026u, among the powers that use the Halvard Gap to move lattice and troops between the spinward and rimward arms without passing Ostrom. Its members are the Gloam Purview, Mercer-Ibarra, Ferrous Consolidated, the Southern League and Kerr-Varga, with Dunmore Rifles fighting alongside them. The Accord has no treaty and no council. What it has is a supply line, and every member's position in the Glassfields War depends on it.

**The trailing traders.** The Free Lines, the Cormorant Regulars, the Long Families and Sable Harbor share an interest in open lanes and low tolls, and they cooperate more than they admit. The Gatherers' Guild sells through them, Rimelight sells to them when the Assay House's price is poor, and the Tallymen live off them. This bloc has the most ships and the least territory.

**Outside all three.** The Synod stands largely apart, as do the Orison Assembly, the Lagoon Towns of Wending and the Bellows Commune. The Compact is at war with the crossroads and armed from outside it, and the Coldwell Claims are at war with the Purview. Corvel works with everyone and is trusted by no one, and the Seventh, the Unlit, the Ringwreckers and Halloran's Heirs answer to nobody.
